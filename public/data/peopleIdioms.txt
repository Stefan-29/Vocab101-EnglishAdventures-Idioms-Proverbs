English Idioms: Descriptions of People
Character, personality traits, appearance & behavior — an idiom module for young learners
Idioms are expressions where the words together mean something different from their normal, literal meaning. In this module, you will learn 67 idioms all about describing people — their personalities, quirks, intelligence, social status, and behavior. Read each meaning and example carefully, then try the practice sentences at the end.
1. Character, Honesty & Mystery
1. bad egg
•	Meaning: An untrustworthy person who is often involved in trouble.
•	Example: "I don't want my son to be friends with Bobby Smith. Bobby's a bad egg."
2. be as good as your word
•	Meaning: To keep your promises and do exactly what you said you would.
•	Example: "Emily promised to help me prepare the food and she was as good as her word."
3. bosom friends
•	Meaning: Very close, trusted friends who confide in each other.
•	Example: "Olivia and Emily have been bosom friends since childhood."
4. a closed book
•	Meaning: Someone who is private, mysterious, or difficult to know or understand.
•	Example: "Julia is something of a closed book. I know nothing about her."
5. an open book
•	Meaning: Someone whose thoughts and feelings are easy to read and clear to everyone.
•	Example: "We could see immediately that she was delighted with the proposal. Her face was an open book."
6. you can't judge a book by its cover
•	Meaning: You should not form an opinion about someone based solely on their appearance.
•	Example: "He leads a very simple life but in fact he's an extremely rich man. You can't judge a book by its cover!"
7. butter wouldn't melt in your mouth
•	Meaning: Looking completely innocent on the outside, but actually capable of being unkind or sneaky.
•	Example: "The boy who stole the purse looked as if butter wouldn't melt in his mouth."
8. call a spade a spade
•	Meaning: To speak openly, directly, and truthfully about things, even when difficult.
•	Example: "What I like about the new manager is that he calls a spade a spade."
9. dark horse
•	Meaning: A quiet or secretive person who hides a surprising talent, skill, or background.
•	Example: "He is such a dark horse. I never knew he was a playwright!"
10. doubting Thomas
•	Meaning: Someone who refuses to believe something without direct proof or seeing it for themselves.
•	Example: "I had to show him my membership card. What a doubting Thomas!"
11. dyed-in-the-wool
•	Meaning: Someone with fixed, uncompromising, and deep-felt beliefs.
•	Example: "Bob and Jane are dyed-in-the-wool ecologists who use only biodegradable products."
12. a fair-weather friend
•	Meaning: A person who is only a friend when times are good, but disappears during hard times.
•	Example: "I thought I could count on Bill, but I've discovered he's just a fair-weather friend."
13. fast talker
•	Meaning: Someone who speaks smoothly and persuasively, but cannot always be trusted.
•	Example: "The salesman was a fast talker who persuaded the lady to buy a new machine."
14. feet of clay
•	Meaning: A hidden flaw, weakness, or character defect in someone who is widely admired.
•	Example: "No one is perfect. Many successful people have feet of clay."
15. fixed in our ways
•	Meaning: Unwilling to change habits, routines, or traditional ways of doing things.
•	Example: "My grandparents are very fixed in their ways and dislike any changes."
16. hairy at the heel
•	Meaning: Untrustworthy, dangerous, or suspect in character.
•	Example: "Rumour has it that the owner of the club is a bit hairy at the heel."
17. hard as nails
•	Meaning: Tough, unsentimental, and showing no sympathy or emotion.
•	Example: "Don't expect any sympathy from him. He's as hard as nails."
2. Intelligence, Experience & Talents
18. all brawn and no brain
•	Meaning: Physically very strong, but lacking intelligence.
•	Example: "He's an impressive player to watch, but he's all brawn and no brain."
19. armchair critic
•	Meaning: Someone who offers advice or criticism based on theory rather than practical experience.
•	Example: "That guy is such an armchair critic — no experience but plenty of advice."
20. armchair traveller
•	Meaning: Someone who enjoys learning about foreign places through books or TV without actually traveling.
•	Example: "A surprising number of adventure books are bought by armchair travellers."
21. a late bloomer
•	Meaning: Someone who takes longer than usual to show their talents, capabilities, or success.
•	Example: "He was quite slow at school but he turned out to be a late bloomer who is now a renowned academic!"
22. bookworm
•	Meaning: Someone who loves books and spends a great deal of time reading.
•	Example: "A book would be the ideal gift. My mother has always been a bookworm."
23. class act
•	Meaning: Someone who is exceptionally skilled, talented, or impressive at what they do.
•	Example: "The dancer's career is just beginning but she's already a class act."
24. full of hot air
•	Meaning: Talking a lot without saying anything useful; full of exaggerated claims or nonsense.
•	Example: "Don't listen to Alex — he's full of hot air!"
25. going places
•	Meaning: Showing talent, ambition, and potential that will lead to future success.
•	Example: "Even at college it was obvious that Paul was going places."
26. have the makings of
•	Meaning: Possessing the underlying qualities and potential needed to become something great.
•	Example: "The teacher says Sarah has the makings of an excellent journalist."
27. head and shoulders above
•	Meaning: Far superior to or much better than others in a group.
•	Example: "The award winner was head and shoulders above the others."
28. old head on young shoulders
•	Meaning: A young person or child who displays the wisdom, maturity, and judgment of an adult.
•	Example: "When she heard Emily warning her brother to stay out of trouble, her mother thought: 'That's an old head on young shoulders.'"
29. in a class of one's own
•	Meaning: Truly unique, unequaled, and far better than anyone else.
•	Example: "As a singer, Maria Callas was in a class of her own."
30. jack of all trades
•	Meaning: Someone who can do many different types of work, but is not a master of any single one.
•	Example: "I'm looking for a skilled worker, not a jack-of-all-trades."
31. just off the boat
•	Meaning: Naive, inexperienced, or unfamiliar with local ways and customs.
•	Example: "How do you expect me to work with a trainee who's just off the boat!"
3. Social Status, Power & Influence
32. abbreviated piece of nothing
•	Meaning: A slang term for someone viewed as completely insignificant, unimportant, or worthless.
•	Example: "Bob calls his new colleague an abbreviated piece of nothing."
33. (of) all stripes
•	Meaning: From many different backgrounds, types, or varieties.
•	Example: "People of all stripes came to visit the exhibition."
34. big cheese
•	Meaning: An important, powerful person with high status in an organization.
•	Example: "Tom's father is a big cheese in the oil industry."
35. big fish in a small pond
•	Meaning: An important or prominent person within a small, limited group or local community.
•	Example: "He enjoys being a big fish in a small pond."
36. born with a silver spoon in one's mouth
•	Meaning: Born into a very rich and privileged family.
•	Example: "She never has to worry about money; she was born with a silver spoon in her mouth."
37. cog in the machine
•	Meaning: A small, minor part of a large organization or system.
•	Example: "The police quickly realised that the suspect was just a cog in the machine."
38. fat cat
•	Meaning: A wealthy, powerful person who is seen as greedy or self-satisfied.
•	Example: "The place was full of fat cats on their big yachts."
39. fifth wheel
•	Meaning: Someone whose presence in a group is unnecessary, making them feel awkward or useless.
•	Example: "Everyone seemed to have a specific role except me. I felt like a fifth wheel."
40. fur coat and no knickers
•	Meaning: Someone who tries to look high-class or wealthy on the surface, but lacks true sophistication.
•	Example: "Don't let her impress you. She's what we call 'fur coat and no knickers'!"
4. Attitude, Behavior & Energy
41. afraid of one's own shadow
•	Meaning: Excessively timid, easily frightened, or nervous about everything.
•	Example: "I've never seen anyone so easily scared. She's afraid of her own shadow!"
42. all sizzle and no steak
•	Meaning: Disappointing; someone or something that promises a lot but delivers very little substance.
•	Example: "Because of his unkept promises, people refer to the new leader as 'all sizzle and no steak'."
43. all things to all people
•	Meaning: Trying to please or satisfy everyone at all times.
•	Example: "She's exhausted trying to be a good wife, mother, and teacher — she can't be all things to all people."
44. apple of your eye
•	Meaning: Someone (usually a child) who is deeply loved and cherished above all others.
•	Example: "My grandson is the apple of my eye."
45. cat's whiskers (or cat's pyjamas)
•	Meaning: Someone who believes they are superior, exceptionally stylish, or better than everyone else.
•	Example: "Ever since she got a promotion, she thinks she's the cat's whiskers!"
46. (a) copycat
•	Meaning: Someone who copies or imitates the actions, choices, or style of another person.
•	Example: "You’ve chosen the same activity as me — copycat!"
47. dog in the manger
•	Meaning: A person who prevents others from enjoying something that they themselves cannot or do not want to use.
•	Example: "Lisa hates the guitar so she won't allow her son to learn to play it — a real dog in the manger!"
48. eager beaver
•	Meaning: An overly enthusiastic, hardworking person who is always keen to do tasks.
•	Example: "The new accountant works all the time. He's a real eager beaver!"
49. early bird
•	Meaning: A person who likes to get up early or arrive before others.
•	Example: "I’ve always been an early bird — up at 6 am at the latest!"
50. full of beans
•	Meaning: Lively, active, healthy, and high-spirited.
•	Example: "He may be getting old but he's still full of beans."
51. full of piss and vinegar
•	Meaning: Full of youthful energy, boisterous vitality, and enthusiasm.
•	Example: "I had to look after a group of kids full of piss and vinegar."
52. full of the milk of human kindness
•	Meaning: Exceptionally kind, gentle, and compassionate toward others.
•	Example: "She's a wonderful person — full of the milk of human kindness."
53. glutton for punishment
•	Meaning: Someone who eagerly accepts difficult, painful, or unpleasant tasks repeatedly.
•	Example: "You get seasick but you're going to travel by boat — you're a glutton for punishment!"
54. happy camper
•	Meaning: A contented, satisfied person with no complaints.
•	Example: "With his new job and his new car, Andy is a happy camper."
55. happy-go-lucky
•	Meaning: Carefree, cheerful, and untroubled by worries about the future.
•	Example: "Charlie's a happy-go-lucky sort of guy — always in good humour."
56. high and mighty
•	Meaning: Behaving in a haughty, arrogant, or self-important way.
•	Example: "Don't get all high and mighty! Everyone helps with the housework here."
57. holier-than-thou
•	Meaning: Acting morally superior or virtuous compared to others.
•	Example: "I can't stand the holier-than-thou attitude of some political candidates."
5. Appearance, Resemblance & Health
58. chip off the old block
•	Meaning: Someone who closely resembles one of their parents in looks, character, or behavior.
•	Example: "James is a chip off the old block — he reacts the same way as his father."
59. cut from the same cloth
•	Meaning: Two people who are very similar in character, values, or temperament.
•	Example: "Although the brothers look alike, they are not cut from the same cloth."
60. down at heel
•	Meaning: Unkempt, untidy, or wearing shabby clothes due to lack of money.
•	Example: "The down-at-heel student I first met became a successful writer."
61. down to earth
•	Meaning: Practical, sensible, realistic, and unpretentious.
•	Example: "Don't ask Suzy for help. She's fun, but not very down to earth."
62. dressed to kill / dressed up to the nines
•	Meaning: Wearing extremely glamorous, stylish, or formal clothes to attract attention.
•	Example: "Caroline must be going to a party — she's dressed up to the nines."
63. face like a bulldog chewing a wasp / face like thunder / face like a wet week-end
•	Meaning: Expressions describing severe facial looks: screwed-up/unattractive, furiously angry, or sad/miserable.
•	Example: "What's wrong with Pete? He's got a face like a wet week-end!"
64. fresh as a daisy
•	Meaning: Looking clean, energetic, bright, and well-rested.
•	Example: "I met Molly the other day. She looked as fresh as a daisy."
65. go/run to seed
•	Meaning: To neglect one's health, fitness, or appearance over time.
•	Example: "The former athlete has sadly run to seed and is now fat and unhealthy."
66. good as gold
•	Meaning: Well-behaved, obedient, and helpful (especially used for children).
•	Example: "Your children are always as good as gold when I look after them."
67. hale and hearty
•	Meaning: Exceptionally healthy, active, and strong (especially used for elderly people).
•	Example: "My grandmother is still hale and hearty at the age of ninety."
