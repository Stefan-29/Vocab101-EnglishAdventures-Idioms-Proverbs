Commonly-Used Proverbs Beginning With 'B'
Kindness, choices, patience & everyday wisdom — a proverb module for young learners
Proverbs are short, well-known sayings that give advice or share a truth about life, often passed down for generations. In this module, you will learn 39 proverbs that start with the letter B, grouped by theme — judging character, friendship, patience & timing, money & work, resilience & caution, and general wisdom. Read each meaning and example carefully, then try the practice sentences at the end.
Judging People & Character
1. A bad penny always turns up.
Meaning: An unwanted or disreputable person constantly comes back.
Example: "He never returns what he borrows, and yet he keeps showing up asking for more — a bad penny always turns up."
2. A bad tree does not yield good apples.
Meaning: A bad parent does not raise good children.
Example: "He never disciplined his kids, and now they cause trouble everywhere — a bad tree does not yield good apples."
3. A bad workman blames his tools.
Meaning: Blaming tools for bad workmanship is an excuse for lack of skill.
Example: "She blamed the recipe for her burnt cookies, but a bad workman blames his tools."
4. A barking dog seldom bites.
Meaning: Someone who constantly makes threats rarely carries them out.
Example: "My little brother threatens to tell Mom everything, but he never actually does — a barking dog seldom bites."
5. Beauty is only skin deep.
Meaning: A person's character is more important than their appearance.
Example: "She's not the flashiest dresser, but everyone loves her because she's kind — beauty is only skin deep."
6. Beauty is in the eye of the beholder.
Meaning: Different people have different tastes.
Example: "I thought the mural was messy, but my friend called it amazing — beauty is in the eye of the beholder."
7. Beware of Greeks bearing gifts.
Meaning: Don't trust your enemies.
Example: "The rival team "kindly" offered to lend us their old equipment before the big match — beware of Greeks bearing gifts."
8. A black plum is as sweet as a white.
Meaning: People should not be judged by their appearance.
Example: "The new student dressed differently from everyone else, but he turned out to be the kindest in class — a black plum is as sweet as a white."
9. Blood will out.
Meaning: A person's background or education will eventually show.
Example: "He tried to act calm, but his short temper showed through at the game — blood will out."
Friendship & Relationships
10. Better be alone than in bad company.
Meaning: Be careful in the choice of the people you associate with.
Example: "She left the group that kept getting into trouble, deciding it was better be alone than in bad company."
11. Birds of a feather flock together.
Meaning: People of the same sort are usually found together.
Example: "All the kids who love drawing ended up in art club together — birds of a feather flock together."
12. Blood is thicker than water.
Meaning: Family relationships are stronger than relationships with other people.
Example: "Even after their argument, the sisters helped each other first — blood is thicker than water."
13. A broken friendship may be soldered but it will never be sound.
Meaning: Friendships can be rebuilt after a dispute but will never be as strong as before.
Example: "They made up after the big fight, but things felt different between them — a broken friendship may be soldered but it will never be sound."
Patience, Timing & Speech
14. Be slow to promise, quick to fulfil.
Meaning: Be reliable: think carefully before making a promise, then keep it.
Example: "She thought carefully before agreeing to help, then finished the job early — be slow to promise, quick to fulfil."
15. The best advice is found on the pillow.
Meaning: A good night's sleep may help us find an answer to our problem.
Example: "He couldn't solve the tricky math problem at night, but the answer came to him after a good sleep — the best advice is found on the pillow."
16. Better late than never.
Meaning: It's better to do something, even if it's late, than not do it at all.
Example: "He finished his science project a day after it was due, but at least he turned it in — better late than never."
17. Be swift to hear, slow to speak.
Meaning: Listen carefully before speaking.
Example: "She listened to both sides of the argument before saying anything — be swift to hear, slow to speak."
18. Better the devil you know than the devil you don't know.
Meaning: It's better to deal with someone difficult but familiar than change and risk dealing with somebody worse.
Example: "She stuck with her old, strict teacher instead of switching to an unknown one — better the devil you know than the devil you don't know."
Money, Work & Everyday Life
19. Be just before you are generous.
Meaning: Make sure all your debts are paid before you start offering help to others.
Example: "He paid back his sister the money he owed before buying gifts for his friends — be just before you are generous."
20. Beggars can't be choosers.
Meaning: You should not reject an offer if you have no other choice.
Example: "She was thankful for the hand-me-down bike, even though it wasn't her favorite color — beggars can't be choosers."
21. The best helping hand is at the end of your sleeve.
Meaning: The best way to get something done is to do it yourself.
Example: "Instead of waiting for someone else to fix his bike, he learned to do it himself — the best helping hand is at the end of your sleeve."
22. The best things in life are free.
Meaning: You don't have to pay for what is important: happiness, friendship, good health.
Example: "Playing tag with friends in the park made her happier than any toy — the best things in life are free."
23. Better an egg today than a hen tomorrow.
Meaning: It is preferable to have something sure now than the possibility of something better later.
Example: "She took the small prize she'd already won instead of risking it for a bigger one — better an egg today than a hen tomorrow."
24. Better to drink the milk than to eat the cow.
Meaning: Be careful not to destroy the source of your income or welfare.
Example: "He saved his allowance instead of spending it all on candy — better to drink the milk than to eat the cow."
25. A bird in hand is worth two in a bush.
Meaning: It's better to keep what you have than to risk losing it by searching for something else.
Example: "She kept the seat she already had instead of chasing a better one across the room — a bird in hand is worth two in a bush."
26. A book holds a house of gold.
Meaning: There is a wealth of knowledge in books.
Example: "The librarian always says a book holds a house of gold, encouraging everyone to read more."
27. Better brain than brawn.
Meaning: It's better to have a brain than muscles.
Example: "The smallest player figured out the winning strategy — better brain than brawn."
Resilience, Effort & Caution
28. Better be the head of a dog than the tail of a lion.
Meaning: It's better to be the leader of a small group than a subordinate in a bigger one.
Example: "He'd rather lead the small chess club than sit quietly on the big soccer team — better be the head of a dog than the tail of a lion."
29. Better flatter a fool than fight him.
Meaning: It's better to avoid disputes with stupid people.
Example: "She just agreed with her silly cousin instead of arguing — better flatter a fool than fight him."
30. Better lose the saddle than the horse.
Meaning: It's better to stop and accept a small loss than continue and risk losing everything.
Example: "He gave up his seat on the team to avoid losing the whole game for everyone — better lose the saddle than the horse."
31. Better safe than sorry.
Meaning: It's better to be too careful than to be careless and regret it later.
Example: "She wore her helmet even for a short bike ride — better safe than sorry."
32. Better be untaught than ill-taught.
Meaning: It's better not to be taught at all than to be taught badly.
Example: "He waited for the real coach instead of learning bad habits from an untrained one — better be untaught than ill-taught."
33. A burden of one's own choice is not felt.
Meaning: Something difficult seems easier when it is done voluntarily.
Example: "She didn't mind practicing extra since it was her own idea — a burden of one's own choice is not felt."
34. A burnt child dreads the fire.
Meaning: A bad experience will make people stay away from certain things.
Example: "After touching the hot pan once, she's always careful in the kitchen now — a burnt child dreads the fire."
35. A bad cause requires many words.
Meaning: It is difficult to defend a bad cause.
Example: "He kept making excuses for breaking the rule, but a bad cause requires many words."
Wise Sayings & General Truths
36. Bad beginnings lead to bad results.
Meaning: If you start something badly the result will be unsatisfactory.
Example: "He rushed through the instructions and the project fell apart — bad beginnings lead to bad results."
37. A bad excuse is better than none.
Meaning: Always give an excuse when necessary, even if it's a poor one.
Example: "He mumbled something about the bus being late — a bad excuse is better than none."
38. Bad news travels fast.
Meaning: People tend to circulate bad news very quickly (accidents, illness, etc.).
Example: "Within minutes, the whole school knew about the broken window — bad news travels fast."
39. Beauty is the wisdom of women. Wisdom is the beauty of men.
Meaning: A playful old rhyme suggesting that grace is a woman's wisdom, and wisdom is a man's grace.
Example: "Grandpa always says this old rhyme with a wink, since it's more of a fun saying than a strict rule."
