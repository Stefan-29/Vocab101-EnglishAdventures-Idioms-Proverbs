Commonly-Used Proverbs Beginning With 'A'
Wisdom, friendship, patience & everyday life — a proverb module for young learners
Proverbs are short, well-known sayings that give advice or share a truth about life, often passed down for generations. In this module, you will learn 115 proverbs that start with the letter A, grouped by theme — judging people's character, friendship, patience, money, everyday life, facing hardship, and general wisdom. Read each meaning and example carefully, then try the practice sentences at the end.
Judging People & Character
1. A bad tree does not yield good apples.
Meaning: Parents who behave badly often raise children who behave badly too.
Example: "The coach reminded parents that a bad tree does not yield good apples, so they should set a good example."
2. A bad workman blames his tools.
Meaning: People with poor skills often blame their equipment instead of themselves.
Example: "He blamed his bike for losing the race, but a bad workman blames his tools."
3. A barking dog seldom bites.
Meaning: People who make a lot of threats rarely actually do what they threaten.
Example: "Don't worry about his shouting — a barking dog seldom bites."
4. A black plum is as sweet as a white.
Meaning: You shouldn't judge someone by how they look.
Example: "The new student looked different, but remember: a black plum is as sweet as a white."
5. A friend's eye is a good mirror.
Meaning: A true friend will tell you the honest truth about yourself.
Example: "She told me my project needed work — a friend's eye is a good mirror."
6. A friend to all is a friend to none.
Meaning: Someone who tries to be everyone's best friend ends up being truly close to no one.
Example: "He's nice to everyone, but a friend to all is a friend to none — he has no close friends."
7. A good name is better than a good face.
Meaning: Having a good reputation matters more than looking good.
Example: "Grandpa always said a good name is better than a good face."
8. A guilty conscience needs no accuser.
Meaning: If you know you did something wrong, you don't need someone else to point it out.
Example: "Nobody had to say anything — a guilty conscience needs no accuser, and he apologized right away."
9. A good conscience is a soft pillow.
Meaning: You sleep peacefully when you haven't done anything wrong.
Example: "She always tells the truth because a good conscience is a soft pillow."
10. A leopard cannot change its spots.
Meaning: People rarely change their basic nature.
Example: "He's always been mischievous — a leopard cannot change its spots."
11. A man is judged by his deeds, not by his words.
Meaning: People are judged by what they actually do, not by what they say.
Example: "Talk is easy, but a man is judged by his deeds, not by his words."
12. A man is known by the company he keeps.
Meaning: A person's character is shown by the friends they choose.
Example: "Choose your friends wisely, because a man is known by the company he keeps."
13. A monkey in silk is a monkey no less.
Meaning: Wearing nice clothes doesn't change who you really are underneath.
Example: "Fancy clothes don't fool anyone — a monkey in silk is a monkey no less."
14. A rotten apple spoils the barrel.
Meaning: One dishonest or badly-behaved person can have a bad influence on a whole group.
Example: "The teacher separated the troublemaker, since a rotten apple spoils the barrel."
15. A wise head keeps a still tongue.
Meaning: Intelligent people know when it's best to stay quiet.
Example: "She listened carefully and said nothing, because a wise head keeps a still tongue."
16. A bad penny always turns up.
Meaning: An unwelcome person or thing tends to keep reappearing.
Example: "That old rumor is back again — a bad penny always turns up."
17. All cats are grey in the dark.
Meaning: People aren't easily told apart until they've proven themselves and stand out.
Example: "In the new class, all cats are grey in the dark until everyone gets to know each other."
18. Actions speak louder than words.
Meaning: What you do matters more than what you say.
Example: "He promised to help, but actions speak louder than words, so we'll see if he actually shows up."
19. An old fox is not easily snared.
Meaning: An experienced person is hard to trick.
Example: "The veteran chess player saw the trap coming — an old fox is not easily snared."
20. Admiration is the daughter of ignorance.
Meaning: Sometimes people admire someone so much that they refuse to see that person's faults.
Example: "His fans wouldn't hear a bad word about him — admiration is the daughter of ignorance."
21. A fool at forty is a fool forever.
Meaning: If someone hasn't matured or learned good sense by a certain age, they probably never will.
Example: "Grandpa laughed and said a fool at forty is a fool forever."
22. A man is as old as he feels himself to be.
Meaning: Age is less important than how healthy and lively you feel.
Example: "Even at seventy, she still hikes every weekend — a man is as old as he feels himself to be."
Friendship & Relationships
23. A broken friendship may be soldered but will never be sound.
Meaning: A friendship can be repaired after an argument, but it may never feel quite the same again.
Example: "They made up after the fight, but a broken friendship may be soldered but will never be sound."
24. A constant guest is never welcome.
Meaning: Visiting too often can make people less happy to see you.
Example: "He stopped dropping by every single day, remembering that a constant guest is never welcome."
25. A friend in need is a friend indeed.
Meaning: A true friend is someone who helps you when you're in trouble.
Example: "She helped me finish the project when I was stuck — a friend in need is a friend indeed."
26. A hedge between keeps friendship green.
Meaning: Respecting each other's privacy helps a friendship stay strong.
Example: "They give each other space, because a hedge between keeps friendship green."
27. A real friend is one who walks in when the rest of the world walks out.
Meaning: A true friend stays by your side even when things are difficult.
Example: "When everyone else stopped calling, she didn't — a real friend is one who walks in when the rest of the world walks out."
28. A problem shared is a problem halved.
Meaning: Talking about a problem with someone else makes it easier to handle.
Example: "I told my sister about my worry, and a problem shared is a problem halved."
29. Absence makes the heart grow fonder.
Meaning: Being apart from someone you love can make you appreciate them even more.
Example: "After a month away at camp, she missed her family so much — absence makes the heart grow fonder."
30. Absence is the mother of disillusion.
Meaning: Being away from someone for a long time can make you see them more clearly, and not always favorably.
Example: "After moving away, he realized his old friend wasn't as kind as he remembered — absence is the mother of disillusion."
31. Adversity makes strange bedfellows.
Meaning: People who wouldn't normally get along can be brought together by facing the same hardship.
Example: "The two rival teams worked together after the storm — adversity makes strange bedfellows."
32. A loveless life is living death.
Meaning: A life without love or affection feels empty and difficult.
Example: "The old story warned that a loveless life is living death."
33. Age before beauty.
Meaning: Older people are sometimes given priority ahead of younger people.
Example: "He held the door and joked, 'Age before beauty!'"
Patience & Timing
34. A watched pot never boils.
Meaning: Time seems to pass very slowly when you're anxiously waiting for something.
Example: "Stop staring at the oven — a watched pot never boils!"
35. A stitch in time saves nine.
Meaning: Fixing a small problem right away saves a lot more trouble later.
Example: "Sew that small tear now — a stitch in time saves nine."
36. A day of sorrow is longer than a month of joy.
Meaning: Time feels like it passes quickly when you're happy and slowly when you're sad.
Example: "The week she was sick felt endless — a day of sorrow is longer than a month of joy."
37. All things grow with time — except grief.
Meaning: As time passes, sadness gradually becomes easier to bear.
Example: "It still hurts, but all things grow with time — except grief, which fades slowly."
38. All in good time.
Meaning: Be patient; things will happen when the moment is right.
Example: "You'll learn to ride without training wheels — all in good time."
39. All good things come to those who wait.
Meaning: Being patient is often rewarded.
Example: "She waited months for the concert tickets, and all good things come to those who wait."
40. A wonder lasts but nine days.
Meaning: Even amazing news is only exciting for a little while before life goes back to normal.
Example: "The class was thrilled about the new pet hamster, but a wonder lasts but nine days."
41. Any time means no time.
Meaning: If you don't set a specific date for something, it often never happens.
Example: "'We'll hang out sometime' usually means never — any time means no time."
42. All days are short to industry and long to idleness.
Meaning: Time flies when you're busy, but drags when you have nothing to do.
Example: "The afternoon crawled by until we found something to do — all days are short to industry and long to idleness."
43. Always in a hurry, always behind.
Meaning: Rushing to do things quickly often makes you less efficient and even later.
Example: "He rushed and forgot his homework — always in a hurry, always behind."
44. A journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step.
Meaning: Even the biggest task starts with one small first action.
Example: "Start with page one — a journey of a thousand miles begins with a single step."
45. A rolling stone gathers no moss.
Meaning: Someone who keeps moving around doesn't build up lasting friendships or belongings — though some see this as a way to stay free.
Example: "He's lived in five cities already — a rolling stone gathers no moss."
46. All things are difficult before they are easy.
Meaning: Everything feels hard at first, but gets easier with practice.
Example: "Riding a bike felt impossible at first, but all things are difficult before they are easy."
47. A danger foreseen is half avoided.
Meaning: If you prepare for a problem in advance, it becomes much easier to handle.
Example: "We packed extra water for the hike — a danger foreseen is half avoided."
48. A stumble may prevent a fall.
Meaning: A small mistake can teach you a lesson that helps you avoid a bigger one.
Example: "Tripping on the first step made her more careful — a stumble may prevent a fall."
49. An ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure.
Meaning: It's easier to prevent a problem than to fix it after it happens.
Example: "Wear your helmet — an ounce of prevention is worth a pound of cure."
50. Advice is least heeded when most needed.
Meaning: When people are in serious trouble, they often ignore the advice that could help them most.
Example: "He wouldn't listen even when things got worse — advice is least heeded when most needed."
Money & Work
51. A fool and his money are (soon) easily parted.
Meaning: A careless person spends money quickly and without much thought.
Example: "He spent his whole allowance in a day — a fool and his money are soon parted."
52. A happy heart is better than a full purse.
Meaning: Being happy is more valuable than having a lot of money.
Example: "They may not be rich, but they're joyful — a happy heart is better than a full purse."
53. A heavy purse gives to a light heart.
Meaning: Having enough money can make you feel more relaxed and secure.
Example: "After saving up, she felt so relieved — a heavy purse gives to a light heart."
54. A loaded wagon makes no noise.
Meaning: Truly wealthy people usually don't brag about their money.
Example: "The quiet millionaire never mentioned his fortune — a loaded wagon makes no noise."
55. An empty purse frightens away friends.
Meaning: When someone runs low on money, some friends may disappear.
Example: "He noticed fewer invitations after losing his job — an empty purse frightens away friends."
56. A young idler, an old beggar.
Meaning: If you don't work hard when you're young, you may struggle financially later in life.
Example: "Grandpa always reminded us: a young idler, an old beggar."
57. Another day, another dollar.
Meaning: A job might be tiring, but at least it brings some income.
Example: "He shrugged and said, 'Another day, another dollar,' before heading to work."
58. Advice is cheap.
Meaning: It costs nothing to tell someone what to do.
Example: "Everyone had an opinion, but advice is cheap."
59. Advisors run no risks.
Meaning: It's easy to tell someone else what to do, since you're not the one taking the risk.
Example: "He criticized our plan from the sidelines — advisors run no risks."
60. All covet, all lose.
Meaning: Trying to grab everything can end up costing you everything.
Example: "He wanted every prize and ended up winning none — all covet, all lose."
61. A bird in hand is worth two in a bush.
Meaning: It's better to keep what you already have than risk it chasing something better.
Example: "She kept her steady job instead of a risky offer — a bird in hand is worth two in a bush."
62. Neither a lender nor a borrower be.
Meaning: It's wise to avoid lending or borrowing money.
Example: "He never mixes money and friendship — neither a lender nor a borrower be."
Health, Nature & Everyday Life
63. A burnt child dreads the fire.
Meaning: A painful experience makes people cautious about similar things in the future.
Example: "After touching the hot pan once, she's very careful now — a burnt child dreads the fire."
64. A cat has nine lives.
Meaning: Cats seem to survive dangerous falls and accidents again and again.
Example: "The cat fell from the roof and walked away fine — a cat has nine lives."
65. An apple a day keeps the doctor away.
Meaning: Eating healthy food regularly can help keep you well.
Example: "She always packs fruit for lunch — an apple a day keeps the doctor away."
66. An onion a day keeps everyone away.
Meaning: A joking twist on the apple saying, about something with a strong smell keeping people at a distance.
Example: "He joked that eating onion for lunch would keep everyone away."
67. April showers bring May flowers.
Meaning: Difficult times now can lead to good things later.
Example: "The hard week of studying paid off in the end — April showers bring May flowers."
68. A dry March, a wet April and a cool May fill barn and cellar and bring much hay.
Meaning: Certain weather patterns are believed to predict a good harvest.
Example: "The farmer smiled at the weather, remembering the old saying about March, April, and May."
69. A growing youth has a wolf in his belly.
Meaning: Growing kids and teens often seem to be hungry all the time.
Example: "He asked for a third snack — a growing youth has a wolf in his belly!"
70. A hungry belly has no ears.
Meaning: When you're very hungry, it's hard to think about anything except food.
Example: "He couldn't focus on the lesson before lunch — a hungry belly has no ears."
71. A hungry wolf is fixed to no place.
Meaning: Someone in need will keep searching different places until they find what they're looking for.
Example: "He tried three shops looking for the toy — a hungry wolf is fixed to no place."
72. An ant may well destroy a whole dam.
Meaning: A tiny problem, if ignored, can cause serious damage.
Example: "That small leak became a big issue — an ant may well destroy a whole dam."
73. A swallow does not make the summer.
Meaning: One good sign doesn't mean everything is fine.
Example: "One good grade doesn't mean he's caught up — a swallow does not make the summer."
74. A tidy house holds a bored woman.
Meaning: If a house is always perfectly clean, it might mean its owner has nothing else to do.
Example: "The old saying joked that a tidy house holds a bored woman."
75. An idle brain is the devil's workshop.
Meaning: Staying busy helps you avoid getting into trouble.
Example: "Mom signed me up for chess club — an idle brain is the devil's workshop."
76. All work and no play makes Jack a dull boy.
Meaning: Everyone needs some rest and fun, not just constant work.
Example: "Take a break and relax — all work and no play makes Jack a dull boy."
77. After dinner rest a while, after supper walk a while.
Meaning: It's healthy to rest after a big midday meal and take a walk after a lighter evening meal.
Example: "Grandma always follows the old rule: after dinner rest a while, after supper walk a while."
78. A fly will not get into a closed mouth.
Meaning: Staying quiet at the right moment helps you avoid trouble.
Example: "She bit her tongue during the argument — a fly will not get into a closed mouth."
79. A little of what you fancy does you good.
Meaning: It's okay to enjoy a small treat now and then.
Example: "One cookie won't hurt — a little of what you fancy does you good."
Resilience, Effort & Adversity
80. A calm sea does not make a skilled sailor.
Meaning: You only prove your skill when things get difficult.
Example: "She handled the tough exam questions well — a calm sea does not make a skilled sailor."
81. A smooth sea never made a skilled mariner.
Meaning: Facing challenges is how people become truly capable.
Example: "He grew so much from that hard project — a smooth sea never made a skilled mariner."
82. Adversity and loss make a man wise.
Meaning: We often learn valuable lessons through hard times.
Example: "After a tough year, she felt she'd grown wiser — adversity and loss make a man wise."
83. A chain is no stronger than its weakest link.
Meaning: A team or group is only as strong as its weakest member.
Example: "Everyone needs to practice, since a chain is no stronger than its weakest link."
84. A change is as good as a rest.
Meaning: Doing something different can feel just as refreshing as taking a break.
Example: "Switching from math to art homework felt refreshing — a change is as good as a rest."
85. A burden of one's own choice is not felt.
Meaning: A hard task feels lighter when you choose to do it yourself.
Example: "She didn't mind the extra practice since it was her own choice — a burden of one's own choice is not felt."
86. A little fire is quickly trodden out.
Meaning: Dealing with a small problem right away stops it from becoming a big one.
Example: "We fixed the small disagreement fast — a little fire is quickly trodden out."
87. A fault confessed is half redressed.
Meaning: Admitting your mistake is the first step to making things right.
Example: "He apologized right away — a fault confessed is half redressed."
88. A flower blooms more than once.
Meaning: If you miss a chance, another opportunity will likely come again.
Example: "Don't worry about missing tryouts — a flower blooms more than once."
89. A bad excuse is better than none.
Meaning: It's better to offer some explanation, even a weak one, than none at all.
Example: "He mumbled a reason for being late — a bad excuse is better than none."
90. A rising tide lifts all boats.
Meaning: When things improve generally, everyone benefits.
Example: "When the whole class improved, everyone felt proud — a rising tide lifts all boats."
91. A man can die but once.
Meaning: Since death only happens once, some things are worth facing bravely.
Example: "The old saying reminds us that a man can die but once."
92. Accidents will happen.
Meaning: Some mishaps are simply unavoidable, no matter how careful we are.
Example: "The vase broke by accident — well, accidents will happen."
93. Anger is the one thing made better by delay.
Meaning: It's often best to wait before reacting when you're angry.
Example: "She took a breath before responding — anger is the one thing made better by delay."
94. Agree, for the law is costly.
Meaning: It's often better to settle a disagreement than go through an expensive dispute.
Example: "The neighbors decided to agree, for the law is costly."
95. A handful of patience is worth more than a bushel of brains.
Meaning: Being patient is often more valuable than being clever.
Example: "She stayed calm and figured it out slowly — a handful of patience is worth more than a bushel of brains."
Wise Sayings & General Truths
96. A book holds a house of gold.
Meaning: Books contain a wealth of valuable knowledge.
Example: "The librarian always says a book holds a house of gold."
97. A broken watch is right once a day.
Meaning: Even someone who is usually wrong can occasionally be correct.
Example: "He guessed right for once — even a broken watch is right once a day."
98. A drop of ink may make a million think.
Meaning: A single written idea can influence a huge number of people.
Example: "Her article changed how people saw the issue — a drop of ink may make a million think."
99. A picture paints a thousand words.
Meaning: An image can express more than a long description.
Example: "The photo said it all — a picture paints a thousand words."
100. A soft answer turneth away wrath.
Meaning: Responding calmly and kindly can prevent an argument from growing.
Example: "She stayed polite, and the tension disappeared — a soft answer turneth away wrath."
101. A nod is as good as a wink (to a blind horse/man).
Meaning: A small hint is enough for someone who already understands.
Example: "I just raised an eyebrow, and she got it — a nod is as good as a wink."
102. A new broom sweeps clean (but the old brush knows all the corners).
Meaning: New people often bring quick changes, but experience is valuable too.
Example: "The new captain changed the drills right away — a new broom sweeps clean."
103. A good beginning makes a good end.
Meaning: Careful planning at the start often leads to good results.
Example: "We outlined the project first — a good beginning makes a good end."
104. A good example is the best sermon.
Meaning: Showing good behavior teaches better than just talking about it.
Example: "The coach always plays fair — a good example is the best sermon."
105. A good mind possesses a kingdom.
Meaning: Knowledge and intelligence are more valuable than material wealth.
Example: "She'd rather read than shop — a good mind possesses a kingdom."
106. All that glitters is not gold.
Meaning: Things that look impressive aren't always as good as they seem.
Example: "The shiny toy broke in a day — all that glitters is not gold."
107. All is well that ends well.
Meaning: A happy ending makes up for any troubles along the way.
Example: "We got lost but found the trail eventually — all is well that ends well."
108. All is fair in love and war.
Meaning: In serious competitions or matters of love, unusual tactics are often excused.
Example: "He used a clever trick to win the game — all is fair in love and war."
109. All roads lead to Rome.
Meaning: There are many different paths to reach the same goal.
Example: "You can solve it several ways — all roads lead to Rome."
110. As you sow, so shall you reap.
Meaning: You experience the results of your own actions.
Example: "He studied hard and did well — as you sow, so shall you reap."
111. Ask me no questions, I'll tell you no lies.
Meaning: Some things are better left undiscussed.
Example: "She smiled and said, 'Ask me no questions, I'll tell you no lies.'"
112. An Englishman's home is his castle.
Meaning: A person's home is where they feel safest and most free to do as they wish.
Example: "He loves relaxing at home — an Englishman's home is his castle."
113. An ounce of discretion is worth a pound of wit.
Meaning: Being careful and thoughtful is better than trying to be clever or funny.
Example: "She chose her words carefully — an ounce of discretion is worth a pound of wit."
114. (The) apple doesn't fall far from the tree.
Meaning: Children often turn out similar to their parents.
Example: "She loves music just like her mom — the apple doesn't fall far from the tree."
115. A lie begets a lie.
Meaning: Telling one lie often leads to telling another to cover it up.
Example: "One small lie turned into a big mess — a lie begets a lie."
