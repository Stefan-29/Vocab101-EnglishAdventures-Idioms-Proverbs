English Idioms About Anger, Annoyance, and Irritation
Expressing frustration, rage, and impatience — an idiom module for young learners
Idioms are expressions where the words together mean something different from their normal, literal meaning. In this module, you will learn 68 idioms all about anger, annoyance, and irritation — expressing outbursts, dealing with frustrating situations, losing one's temper, and holding grudges. Read each meaning and example carefully, then try the practice sentences at the end. 
1. Outbursts, Rage & Losing Your Temper
1. all hell broke loose
•	Meaning: A sudden angry, noisy, or chaotic reaction occurs. 
•	Example: "When it was announced that the plant was going to close down, all hell broke loose."
2. blow a fuse
•	Meaning: To suddenly lose your temper and become very angry. 
•	Example: "Charlie blew a fuse yesterday when he discovered that his bike had been stolen."
3. blow a gasket
•	Meaning: To explode with furious anger. 
•	Example: "When the shop was burgled for the third time, the owner blew a gasket."
4. blow your top
•	Meaning: To suddenly become extremely angry. 
•	Example: "When my mother saw the state of the house after the party, she blew her top!"
5. flip your lid
•	Meaning: To become very angry or upset. 
•	Example: "Julie flipped her lid when she saw the state of her daughter's bedroom."
6. fly off the handle
•	Meaning: To suddenly become very angry. 
•	Example: "Dad flew off the handle when I told him I had damaged his new car."
7. foam at the mouth
•	Meaning: To be extremely angry about something. 
•	Example: "The director was foaming at the mouth when he saw a picture of his children in the newspaper."
8. go ballistic
•	Meaning: To become extremely angry. 
•	Example: "My dad went ballistic when he saw the state of the garden after the barbecue."
9. go off the deep end
•	Meaning: To become so angry or upset that you cannot control your emotions. 
•	Example: "Eva will go off the deep end if her kids leave the kitchen in a mess again."
10. go postal
•	Meaning: To lose your temper and express anger in a violent or extreme way. 
•	Example: "My parents will go postal when they see the state of the house!"
11. go spare
•	Meaning: To lose your temper completely. 
•	Example: "Lea's dad would go spare if he knew how much she spent in London!"
12. go through the roof / hit the roof
•	Meaning: To become furious or extremely angry. 
•	Example: "My father went through the roof when Paul damaged his new car."
13. lose your cool
•	Meaning: To behave in a bad-tempered manner or become angry and flustered. 
•	Example: "The customer lost his cool when the waiter spilt wine on his shirt."
14. see red
•	Meaning: To suddenly become very angry or annoyed. 
•	Example: "Discrimination of any kind makes me see red."
15. throw a wobbly (or wobbler)
•	Meaning: To have a fit of nerves or bad temper and lose all self-control. 
•	Example: "He's very calm — not the sort of man to throw a wobbly if he doesn't have a clean shirt!"
16. (a) flare-up
•	Meaning: A sudden outburst of anger or violence. 
•	Example: "There was another flare-up of fighting between the two gangs as soon as the police left the area."
2. Annoyance, Irritation & Nuisances
17. bear with a sore head
•	Meaning: Behaving in a very irritable and bad-tempered manner. 
•	Example: "When his team lost the match, Brad was like a bear with a sore head."
18. (get) bent out of shape
•	Meaning: Becoming angry, agitated, upset, or taking offense at something. 
•	Example: "Dad got bent out of shape when he found his car all scratched in the car park."
19. bete noire
•	Meaning: A person or thing that you dislike, dread, or find extremely annoying. 
•	Example: "My father’s bete noire is cigarette butts crushed on the lawn. That irritates him no end!"
20. (be) in a black mood
•	Meaning: Being irritable, angry, or depressed. 
•	Example: "You'd better keep away from Bill today. He's in a black mood."
21. get someone's goat
•	Meaning: To annoy or irritate someone. 
•	Example: "People who keep pushing when you're standing in line really get my goat!"
22. get in someone's hair
•	Meaning: To annoy someone continuously so they cannot get on with their work. 
•	Example: "I'd finish the report more quickly if my colleague wasn't getting in my hair all the time!"
23. get on someone's nerves
•	Meaning: To annoy or irritate someone a great deal. 
•	Example: "The boys next door are so noisy they're getting on my nerves."
24. hot under the collar
•	Meaning: Feeling annoyed, indignant, or embarrassed. 
•	Example: "If anyone criticizes his proposals, Joe immediately gets hot under the collar."
25. make your hackles rise
•	Meaning: To cause someone to become angry. 
•	Example: "Her constant criticism really makes my hackles rise!"
26. rub someone (up) the wrong way
•	Meaning: To annoy or irritate someone without intending to. 
•	Example: "Be careful not to rub the boss (up) the wrong way if you want a promotion!"
27. (get) steamed up about something
•	Meaning: To become very angry, excited, or agitated about something. 
•	Example: "Calm down — there's no need to get all steamed up about it!"
28. step on someone's toes
•	Meaning: To annoy or irritate someone by interfering in their area of responsibility. 
•	Example: "I could offer some advice, but I'm afraid of stepping on someone's toes."
29. (something) sticks in your throat
•	Meaning: Something that is very difficult to accept and causes anger or resentment. 
•	Example: "The way he treats women really sticks in my throat!"
30. that makes my blood boil!
•	Meaning: Something that makes you extremely angry. 
•	Example: "His condescending attitude towards women really makes my blood boil!"
31. be cheesed off
•	Meaning: Feeling annoyed, bored, or frustrated. 
•	Example: "Jenny is absolutely cheesed off with her job."
32. drive someone up the wall
•	Meaning: To annoy or irritate someone to the point of intolerable distraction. 
•	Example: "I can't concentrate with all the noise — it's driving me up the wall."
33. fit to be tied
•	Meaning: Extremely irritated, upset, or angry. 
•	Example: "Harry was fit to be tied when his dog dug up the flowers he had planted."
3. Verbal Conflict, Scolding & Criticizing
34. bite someone's head off
•	Meaning: To criticize someone strongly or unfairly. 
•	Example: "I worked 10 hours a day all week and my boss bit my head off for not doing my share of the work!"
35. flea in your ear
•	Meaning: Sent away with an angry reprimand or severe rebuke. 
•	Example: "When Andy tried to put the blame on Pete, he was sent away with a flea in his ear."
36. give (someone) a piece of your mind
•	Meaning: To express your thoughts or anger to someone directly and forcefully. 
•	Example: "Jack was so irritated by his neighbours' behaviour that he decided to give them a piece of his mind."
37. give the (rough) edge of your tongue
•	Meaning: To scold someone severely or speak to them very aggressively. 
•	Example: "My boss was so angry that I really got the rough edge of his tongue."
38. give someone a tongue-lashing
•	Meaning: To scold someone severely. 
•	Example: "The teacher gave Jeremy a tongue-lashing when he arrived late for school for the third time."
39. haul (someone) over the coals
•	Meaning: To reprimand someone harshly because they made a mistake. 
•	Example: "Sam was hauled over the coals for the poor quality of his presentation."
40. jump down someone's throat
•	Meaning: To suddenly start shouting at or reacting angrily to someone. 
•	Example: "When I said the instructions were not very clear, she jumped down my throat!"
41. rant and rave
•	Meaning: To protest noisily and forcefully. 
•	Example: "The old man ranted and raved about the new waste collection system, but he had to accept it."
42. scream blue murder
•	Meaning: To shout or complain very loudly as if something severe has occurred. 
•	Example: "The crowd started screaming blue murder when the football match was interrupted."
43. skin someone alive
•	Meaning: A threat to punish someone severely out of anger. 
•	Example: "If that kid damages my car again, I'll skin him alive!"
44. tear a strip off someone
•	Meaning: To reprimand someone severely for doing something wrong. 
•	Example: "The teacher tore a strip off Charlie for not doing his homework."
45. tell someone a thing or two
•	Meaning: To express your criticism or opinions very clearly and directly. 
•	Example: *"Let me tell you a thing or two about your son's behaviour," said John to the boy's father. 
46. come down on someone like a ton of bricks
•	Meaning: To criticize or punish someone severely for a wrongdoing. 
•	Example: "If you mix up the order, the boss will come down on you like a ton of bricks!"
47. eat someone alive
•	Meaning: To criticize or confront someone severely out of anger. 
•	Example: "The boss will eat me alive if the report arrives late."
48. get/take flak
•	Meaning: To receive severe criticism for something you have done. 
•	Example: "Harry got a lot of flak for the way he handled the situation."
4. Hostility, Disagreements & Tensions
49. up in arms
•	Meaning: Being very angry or protesting strongly about something. 
•	Example: "The population was up in arms over the demolition of the old theatre."
50. (have a) bone to pick with someone
•	Meaning: Being annoyed with someone and wanting to discuss it with them. 
•	Example: "Mark wants to see the boss. He says he's got a bone to pick with him."
51. the gloves are off!
•	Meaning: A sign or statement that a serious argument or fight is about to start. 
•	Example: "The two candidates are out of their seats. The gloves are off!"
52. go on a rampage
•	Meaning: Rushing around in a violent way, causing damage, destruction, or chaos. 
•	Example: "Extensive damage was caused yesterday when gangs of aggressive supporters went on a rampage after the match."
53. have it in for someone
•	Meaning: To hold a grudge against someone and be determined to make life difficult for them. 
•	Example: "Judging from his unfriendly attitude, Jason seems to have it in for Andy, but nobody knows why."
54. kick up a fuss (or a stink)
•	Meaning: To create a scene, complain loudly, or protest angrily about something. 
•	Example: "The service was so slow in the restaurant that several customers began to kick up a fuss."
55. like a red flag to a bull
•	Meaning: A statement or action that is sure to make someone very angry. 
•	Example: "Don't mention Tom's promotion to Mike. It would be like a red flag to a bull!"
56. look daggers at someone
•	Meaning: To look at someone with extreme anger or hostility. 
•	Example: "David looked daggers at Paul when he invited his new girlfriend to dance."
57. more heat than light
•	Meaning: A discussion that causes intense anger and emotion without clarifying the issue. 
•	Example: "The meeting held to discuss the problem generated more heat than light. Nothing was resolved!"
58. road rage
•	Meaning: Aggressive driving habits, often leading to violent reactions against other drivers. 
•	Example: "A number of accidents today are a direct result of road rage."
59. a storm is brewing
•	Meaning: Indications that trouble, emotional outbursts, or anger are imminent. 
•	Example: "As soon as we saw Pete's face, we knew that a storm was brewing."
60. want someone's head on a platter
•	Meaning: To be so angry with someone that you demand severe punishment for them. 
•	Example: "He was so angry when he read the article about his family that he wanted the journalist's head on a platter."
5. Reactions, Exclamations & Calming Interventions
61. get off my back!
•	Meaning: Telling someone to stop criticizing, nagging, or finding fault with you. 
•	Example: "Liz, please, get off my back! You've been making comments about my work all morning!"
62. get a rise out of somebody
•	Meaning: To provoke someone into reacting angrily by teasing or irritating them. 
•	Example: "He gets a rise out of his daughter by asking her about her latest diet."
63. give it a rest!
•	Meaning: Asking someone to stop talking continuously, complaining, or annoying you. 
•	Example: "All you talk about is politics. Give it a rest... please!"
64. (be) glad to see the back of someone
•	Meaning: Happy when an unpleasant person leaves. 
•	Example: "The receptionist was glad to see the back of the customer who had been so rude to him."
65. good riddance!
•	Meaning: An expression of relief at being free of an unwanted person or thing. 
•	Example: "Our horrible neighbour has moved house, and all I can say is 'good riddance'!"
66. harp on (about) something
•	Meaning: To tire others by talking endlessly and tediously about a topic. 
•	Example: "My parents are always harping on about my school results."
67. have (or keep) both oars in the water
•	Meaning: Remaining calm, collected, and emotionally stable. 
•	Example: "Stella can deal with any crisis. She always keeps both oars in the water."
68. kick yourself
•	Meaning: Feeling extremely annoyed with yourself for making a mistake or missing an opportunity. 
•	Example: "I could have kicked myself for forgetting Emily's birthday."
