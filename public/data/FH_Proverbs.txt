Commonly-Used Proverbs Beginning With 'F', 'G' & 'H'
Friendship, honesty, effort & everyday wisdom — a proverb module for young learners
Proverbs are short, well-known sayings that give advice or share a truth about life. In this module, you will learn 74 proverbs beginning with F, G, and H, grouped by theme — friendship, honesty and character, effort and success, wisdom and caution, patience and timing, and everyday life. Read each meaning and example carefully, then try the practice sentences at the end.
Friendship
1. False friends are worse than open enemies.
Meaning: It's better to know who your real enemies are than to trust someone who pretends to be your friend but isn't.
Example: "She warned him about the classmate who gossiped behind his back — false friends are worse than open enemies."
2. (A) friend in need is a friend indeed.
Meaning: A true friend is someone who helps you when you're in trouble.
Example: "She helped me finish the project when I was stuck — a friend in need is a friend indeed."
3. (A) friend to all is a friend to none.
Meaning: Someone who tries to be everyone's best friend ends up being truly close to no one.
Example: "He's nice to everyone, but a friend to all is a friend to none."
4. (A) friend's eye is a good mirror.
Meaning: A true friend will tell you the honest truth about yourself.
Example: "She told me my project needed work — a friend's eye is a good mirror."
5. (A) friend is like a mirror.
Meaning: Good friends help us understand ourselves better.
Example: "Talking to my best friend always helps me see things clearly — a friend is like a mirror."
6. (A) real friend is one who walks in when the rest of the world walks out.
Meaning: A true friend stays by your side even when things are difficult.
Example: "When everyone else stopped calling, she didn't — a real friend is one who walks in when the rest of the world walks out."
7. Friendship is like a circle that never ends.
Meaning: True friendship lasts a lifetime.
Example: "Even after moving away, they stayed close — friendship is like a circle that never ends."
8. Friendship is like money, easier made than kept.
Meaning: It's easier to make a new friend than to keep a friendship strong over time.
Example: "They had to work at staying close after graduation — friendship is like money, easier made than kept."
9. Friendship is love with understanding.
Meaning: Friends tend to be more patient and accepting than romantic partners.
Example: "She could tell her friend anything without judgment — friendship is love with understanding."
10. Make new friends but keep the old; one is silver and the other is gold.
Meaning: New friends are valuable, but old friends who have stood the test of time are even more precious.
Example: "She welcomed the new student warmly but still made time for her childhood friend — make new friends but keep the old."
11. Good accounting makes good friends.
Meaning: Being clear and fair about money helps keep a friendship healthy.
Example: "They always split the bill evenly — good accounting makes good friends."
12. (A) hedge between keeps friendship green.
Meaning: Respecting each other's privacy helps a friendship stay strong.
Example: "They give each other space, because a hedge between keeps friendship green."
Honesty, Actions & Character
13. Facts speak louder than words.
Meaning: What people actually do shows their true character better than what they say.
Example: "He promised to help but never showed up — facts speak louder than words."
14. Fine words butter no parsnips.
Meaning: Nice-sounding talk doesn't get anything done — actions matter more.
Example: "He kept making promises, but fine words butter no parsnips."
15. (A) good conscience is a soft pillow.
Meaning: You sleep peacefully when you haven't done anything wrong.
Example: "She always tells the truth because a good conscience is a soft pillow."
16. (A) good name is better than a good face.
Meaning: Having a good reputation matters more than looking good.
Example: "Grandpa always said a good name is better than a good face."
17. Handsome is what handsome does.
Meaning: How you behave matters more than how you look.
Example: "He may not dress fancy, but he's always kind — handsome is what handsome does."
18. Hard words break no bones.
Meaning: Harsh criticism might sting, but it won't really hurt you.
Example: "The coach's tough feedback stung, but hard words break no bones."
19. He who digs a pit for others falls into it himself.
Meaning: If you try to harm others, you often end up harming yourself.
Example: "His plan to trick his friend backfired — he who digs a pit for others falls into it himself."
20. He laughs best who laughs last.
Meaning: Don't celebrate victory too soon — wait until things are truly settled.
Example: "The underdog team ended up winning — he laughs best who laughs last."
21. Honesty is the best policy.
Meaning: It's always better to tell the truth.
Example: "She admitted her mistake right away — honesty is the best policy."
22. Honey catches more flies than vinegar.
Meaning: Being kind gets you more cooperation than being harsh.
Example: "She asked politely instead of demanding, and honey catches more flies than vinegar."
23. (A) guilty conscience needs no accuser.
Meaning: If you know you did something wrong, you don't need anyone else to point it out.
Example: "Nobody had to say anything — a guilty conscience needs no accuser."
Effort, Success & Determination
24. Failure teaches success.
Meaning: We often learn the most important lessons from our mistakes.
Example: "Her first attempt at the science fair flopped, but failure teaches success — she won the next year."
25. Fortune favours the brave / the bold.
Meaning: Good things often come to those who take courageous action.
Example: "She raised her hand to answer the tough question — fortune favours the brave."
26. Gardens are not made by sitting in the shade.
Meaning: You have to put in effort to achieve anything worthwhile.
Example: "He practiced every single day — gardens are not made by sitting in the shade."
27. God helps those who help themselves.
Meaning: Success comes to those who make a real effort, not those who just wait for good luck.
Example: "She studied hard instead of hoping for an easy question — God helps those who help themselves."
28. Good and quickly seldom meet.
Meaning: Doing something well usually takes time.
Example: "He rushed the poster and it showed — good and quickly seldom meet."
29. (A) good beginning makes a good end.
Meaning: Careful planning at the start often leads to good results.
Example: "We outlined the project first — a good beginning makes a good end."
30. Good management is better than good income.
Meaning: Using your resources wisely matters more than how much you earn.
Example: "They live comfortably on a modest salary — good management is better than good income."
31. Grasp all, lose all.
Meaning: Trying to get everything can end up costing you everything.
Example: "He wanted every prize and ended up winning none — grasp all, lose all."
32. Great oaks grow from small acorns.
Meaning: Big achievements often start from small beginnings.
Example: "The school's huge recycling program started with one small idea — great oaks grow from small acorns."
33. He can who believes he can.
Meaning: Believing in yourself is the first step toward succeeding.
Example: "She was nervous about the race, but he can who believes he can — and she finished strong."
34. He who wills the end wills the means.
Meaning: If you're determined enough to reach a goal, you'll find a way to do it.
Example: "He found time to practice even with a busy schedule — he who wills the end wills the means."
Wisdom, Caution & Foolishness
35. (A) fool and his money are (soon) easily parted.
Meaning: A careless person spends money quickly and without much thought.
Example: "He spent his whole allowance in a day — a fool and his money are soon parted."
36. (A) fool at forty is a fool forever.
Meaning: If someone hasn't matured or learned good sense by a certain age, they probably never will.
Example: "Grandpa laughed and said a fool at forty is a fool forever."
37. Fool me once, shame on you; fool me twice, shame on me.
Meaning: If you're tricked once it's not really your fault, but if it happens again, you should have learned from it.
Example: "After falling for the same prank twice, he said, 'Fool me once, shame on you; fool me twice, shame on me.'"
38. Fools rush in where angels fear to tread.
Meaning: Inexperienced people sometimes get involved in situations that wiser people would avoid.
Example: "He jumped straight into the debate without any research — fools rush in where angels fear to tread."
39. Give someone an inch and they will take a mile (or yard).
Meaning: Give someone a small amount of freedom and they may try to take much more.
Example: "She let him stay up ten minutes late, and now he wants an hour — give someone an inch."
40. Give someone enough rope and they will hang themselves.
Meaning: If you give someone enough freedom, their own bad choices will eventually cause them trouble.
Example: "The teacher let him explain his excuse fully — give someone enough rope and they will hang themselves."
41. Great minds think alike.
Meaning: Smart people often come up with the same idea independently.
Example: "We wore matching outfits by accident — great minds think alike!"
42. (A) handful of patience is worth more than a bushel of brains.
Meaning: Being patient is often more valuable than being clever.
Example: "She stayed calm and figured it out slowly — a handful of patience is worth more than a bushel of brains."
43. Haste makes waste.
Meaning: Rushing through something often leads to mistakes that need fixing later.
Example: "He rushed his homework and had to redo it — haste makes waste."
44. He who goes slowly goes safely (and goes far).
Meaning: Being careful and steady is better than rushing.
Example: "She paced herself through the marathon — he who goes slowly goes safely."
45. He who hesitates is lost.
Meaning: If you wait too long to decide, you might miss your chance.
Example: "By the time he decided to sign up, the spots were full — he who hesitates is lost."
46. He who is everywhere is nowhere.
Meaning: Trying to do too many things at once means you won't do any of them well.
Example: "She joined five clubs and couldn't focus on any — he who is everywhere is nowhere."
47. He who knows nothing doubts nothing.
Meaning: People who don't know much don't realize how much there is to question.
Example: "The more she studied, the more questions she had — he who knows nothing doubts nothing."
48. He who plays with fire gets burnt.
Meaning: Taking unnecessary risks usually leads to trouble.
Example: "He kept skipping practice and got benched — he who plays with fire gets burnt."
Patience, Timing & Resolve
49. Fear lends wings
Meaning: Fear can make you move much faster than you normally would.
Example: "He sprinted home in record time when he heard the thunder — fear lends wings."
50. First come, first served.
Meaning: The person who arrives first gets helped first.
Example: "Line up for the free samples — it's first come, first served."
51. (The) first step is the hardest.
Meaning: Starting something new is often the most difficult part.
Example: "Once she started writing, the essay came easily — the first step is the hardest."
52. (A) flower blooms more than once.
Meaning: If you miss a chance, another opportunity will likely come again.
Example: "Don't worry about missing tryouts — a flower blooms more than once."
53. (A) fault confessed is half redressed.
Meaning: Admitting your mistake is the first step to making things right.
Example: "He apologized right away — a fault confessed is half redressed."
54. Grief divided is made lighter.
Meaning: Sharing sadness with others makes it easier to bear.
Example: "She told her friends about her loss, and grief divided is made lighter."
55. Hope for the best, prepare for the worst.
Meaning: Stay optimistic, but also plan in case things don't go well.
Example: "She packed a raincoat just in case — hope for the best, prepare for the worst."
56. However long the night, the dawn will break.
Meaning: No matter how hard things get, difficult times eventually pass.
Example: "She reminded herself that however long the night, the dawn will break."
57. He who pays the piper calls the tune.
Meaning: The person who pays for something gets to decide how it's done.
Example: "Since Dad paid for the trip, he chose where we went — he who pays the piper calls the tune."
58. Half a loaf is better than none
Meaning: It's better to accept something, even if it's less than you hoped for, than to have nothing at all.
Example: "The prize was smaller than expected, but half a loaf is better than none."
59. He has enough who is content.
Meaning: A person who feels satisfied doesn't need anything more.
Example: "He doesn't have much, but he's happy — he has enough who is content."
60. Health is better than wealth.
Meaning: It's more valuable to be healthy than to be rich.
Example: "She turned down the extra shifts to rest — health is better than wealth."
61. Hunger is a good sauce.
Meaning: Food tastes better when you're really hungry.
Example: "After the long hike, even plain crackers tasted amazing — hunger is a good sauce."
Everyday Life & Human Nature
62. Fair exchange is no robbery.
Meaning: Trading two things of equal value is a fair and honest deal.
Example: "They swapped trading cards of equal value — fair exchange is no robbery."
63. Familiarity breeds contempt.
Meaning: Knowing someone very well over time can sometimes lead to less respect for them.
Example: "The roommates started bickering over little things — familiarity breeds contempt."
64. (A) fly will not get into a closed mouth.
Meaning: Staying quiet at the right moment helps you avoid trouble.
Example: "She bit her tongue during the argument — a fly will not get into a closed mouth."
65. Forbidden fruit is sweet.
Meaning: Things seem more appealing when we're not allowed to have them.
Example: "He wanted the video game even more once his parents said no — forbidden fruit is sweet."
66. (A) good mind possesses a kingdom.
Meaning: Intelligence and knowledge are more valuable than material wealth.
Example: "She'd rather read than shop — a good mind possesses a kingdom."
67. (A) growing youth has a wolf in his belly.
Meaning: Growing kids and teens often seem to be hungry all the time.
Example: "He asked for a third snack — a growing youth has a wolf in his belly!"
68. (A) happy heart is better than a full purse.
Meaning: Being happy is more valuable than having a lot of money.
Example: "They may not be rich, but they're joyful — a happy heart is better than a full purse."
69. Hatred is as blind as love.
Meaning: Strong dislike, like strong love, can keep someone from seeing things clearly.
Example: "He couldn't admit his rival had a good idea — hatred is as blind as love."
70. (A) heavy purse gives to a light heart.
Meaning: Having enough money can make you feel more relaxed and secure.
Example: "After saving up, she felt so relieved — a heavy purse gives to a light heart."
71. (The best) helping hand is at the end of your sleeve.
Meaning: The best way to get something done is to do it yourself.
Example: "Instead of waiting for help, she fixed it herself — the best helping hand is at the end of your sleeve."
72. Home is where the heart is.
Meaning: Home is wherever the people you love are.
Example: "Even in a new city, she felt at home with her family nearby — home is where the heart is."
73. (A) hungry belly has no ears.
Meaning: A hungry person can only think about food and nothing else.
Example: "He couldn't focus on the lesson before lunch — a hungry belly has no ears."
74. (A) hungry wolf is fixed to no place.
Meaning: Someone in need will keep searching different places until they find what they're looking for.
Example: "He tried three shops looking for the toy — a hungry wolf is fixed to no place."
