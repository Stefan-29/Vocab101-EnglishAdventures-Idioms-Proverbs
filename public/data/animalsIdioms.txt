English Idioms: Animals, Birds, Fish & Insects
Cats, dogs, bugs, birds & more — an idiom module for young learners
Idioms are expressions where the words together mean something different from their normal, literal meaning. In this module, you will learn 61 idioms that use animal words — insects, birds, bulls and cows, cats, chickens, dogs, and other creatures. Read each meaning and example carefully, then try the practice sentences at the end.
Insects & Small Creatures
1. ants in one's pants
Meaning: Being very restless or excited, unable to sit still.
Example: "She's had ants in her pants all morning waiting for the field trip."
2. eager beaver
Meaning: Someone who works very hard and enthusiastically, sometimes a little too much.
Example: "He's such an eager beaver — he finished his homework before it was even assigned."
3. (as) busy as a bee
Meaning: Very active and full of things to do.
Example: "Mom's been as busy as a bee getting the house ready for the party."
4. have a bee in one's bonnet
Meaning: To have an idea that you keep thinking about constantly.
Example: "He's got a bee in his bonnet about learning to skateboard this summer."
5. the bee's knees
Meaning: Someone or something considered exceptionally good (or, if said about oneself, having a very high opinion of yourself).
Example: "This new game is the bee's knees!"
6. bitten by the bug
Meaning: To suddenly develop a strong interest in something.
Example: "After one lesson, he was bitten by the chess bug."
7. snug as a bug in a rug
Meaning: Very warm, cozy, and comfortable.
Example: "Wrapped in her blanket, she looked as snug as a bug in a rug."
8. social butterfly
Meaning: A person who has lots of friends and enjoys going to many social events.
Example: "Mia is a total social butterfly — she knows everyone at school."
9. (have) butterflies in your stomach
Meaning: To feel very nervous.
Example: "I had butterflies in my stomach before my piano recital."
Birds
10. early bird
Meaning: Someone who likes to get up and start the day early.
Example: "Grandpa's an early bird — he's up gardening by 6 a.m."
11. eat like a bird
Meaning: To eat only small amounts of food.
Example: "My cousin eats like a bird — she barely finishes half her plate."
12. birds of a feather
Meaning: People who are very similar and naturally get along.
Example: "Those two are birds of a feather — always laughing at the same jokes."
13. for the birds
Meaning: Something considered useless, boring, or not worth taking seriously.
Example: "He thought the whole plan was for the birds."
14. kill two birds with one stone
Meaning: To accomplish two things with a single action.
Example: "By walking to school, she killed two birds with one stone — exercise and saving time."
15. (as) dead as a dodo
Meaning: Completely out of date or no longer used at all.
Example: "The old flip phone is as dead as a dodo now."
Bulls, Cows & Farm Animals
16. like a red flag to a bull
Meaning: Something certain to make someone very angry.
Example: "Mentioning the lost game is like a red flag to a bull with him."
17. take the bull by the horns
Meaning: To face a difficult situation directly and with confidence.
Example: "Instead of avoiding the problem, she took the bull by the horns and talked to her teacher."
18. till the cows come home
Meaning: For a very long time.
Example: "You can practice till the cows come home, but you still need a good night's sleep before the game."
19. why buy a cow when you can get milk for free?
Meaning: Why pay for something you can already get without paying.
Example: "He borrows his neighbor's tools instead of buying his own — why buy a cow when you can get milk for free?"
20. make an ass of yourself
Meaning: To behave so foolishly that you look ridiculous.
Example: "He made an ass of himself trying to dance on stage."
21. cash cow
Meaning: A product or activity that regularly earns money.
Example: "The school's snack stand is a real cash cow every football season."
Cats
22. a cat in gloves catches no mice
Meaning: Being too cautious or polite might stop you from getting what you want.
Example: "Negotiate firmly — a cat in gloves catches no mice."
23. a cat can look at a king
Meaning: Even an ordinary person has the right to look at or be curious about someone important.
Example: "The kid stared right at the mayor — well, a cat can look at a king."
24. a fat cat
Meaning: A rich and powerful person, often used disapprovingly.
Example: "The documentary criticized the fat cats running the big companies."
25. let the cat out of the bag
Meaning: To accidentally reveal a secret.
Example: "She let the cat out of the bag about the surprise party."
26. grin like a Cheshire cat
Meaning: To smile very widely because you're happy or pleased about something.
Example: "He grinned like a Cheshire cat when he got his acceptance letter."
27. like a cat that ate the canary
Meaning: Looking very pleased and satisfied with yourself after a success.
Example: "She looked like the cat that ate the canary after acing her exam."
28. like a cat on hot bricks
Meaning: Very nervous and unable to stay still.
Example: "He was like a cat on hot bricks waiting for his test results."
29. like herding cats
Meaning: Very difficult to organize because everyone wants to do their own thing.
Example: "Planning the group trip was like herding cats."
30. like a scalded cat
Meaning: Moving extremely fast, usually out of fear or shock.
Example: "He ran off like a scalded cat when he heard the thunder."
31. like something the cat dragged in
Meaning: Looking messy, dirty, or unkempt.
Example: "After the muddy soccer game, he looked like something the cat dragged in."
32. no room/not enough room to swing a cat
Meaning: A very small, cramped space.
Example: "This closet has no room to swing a cat!"
33. wait for the cat to jump
Meaning: To delay acting until you see how a situation develops.
Example: "Let's wait for the cat to jump before deciding on a plan."
34. cat's lick
Meaning: A very quick, hasty wash.
Example: "He gave himself a cat's lick before rushing out the door."
35. cat's whiskers (also: cat's pyjamas)
Meaning: Someone who believes they are better than others at something.
Example: "Ever since winning the race, she thinks she's the cat's whiskers."
36. a cat-and-dog life
Meaning: A relationship where the people involved argue frequently.
Example: "Those roommates lead a cat-and-dog life — always bickering."
37. raining cats and dogs
Meaning: Raining very heavily.
Example: "We can't go outside — it's raining cats and dogs!"
38. play cat and mouse
Meaning: To alternately treat someone kindly and unkindly, keeping them uncertain.
Example: "The coach kept playing cat and mouse about who'd start the game."
39. set (put) the cat among the pigeons
Meaning: To do or say something that causes trouble or upsets people.
Example: "Announcing the surprise test set the cat among the pigeons."
40. (a) copycat
Meaning: Someone who imitates what another person does.
Example: "Stop being a copycat and pick your own project idea!"
Chickens
41. chicken feed
Meaning: A very small, unimportant amount of money.
Example: "The prize money was just chicken feed compared to the effort put in."
42. chicken out (of)
Meaning: To decide not to do something because you're scared.
Example: "He wanted to try the high dive but chickened out at the last second."
43. like a headless chicken
Meaning: Acting in a panicked, disorganized way without thinking clearly.
Example: "She ran around like a headless chicken looking for her missing shoe."
44. no spring chicken
Meaning: No longer young.
Example: "Grandpa says he's no spring chicken, but he still beats us at chess."
45. wave a dead chicken
Meaning: To do something you know won't really work, just to show you tried.
Example: "The technician waved a dead chicken to reassure the customer before explaining the real fix was needed."
46. chickens come home to roost
Meaning: Past bad actions eventually cause problems for the person who did them.
Example: "He never studied all year, and now the chickens have come home to roost before finals."
Dogs
47. face like a bulldog chewing a wasp
Meaning: Having a very unpleasant, scrunched-up facial expression.
Example: "He had a face like a bulldog chewing a wasp when he tasted the sour candy."
48. a dog's breakfast
Meaning: A complete mess.
Example: "The group project turned into a real dog's breakfast."
49. a dog's life
Meaning: An unpleasant or hard way of living.
Example: "Working every weekend felt like a dog's life."
50. dog eat dog
Meaning: Describes a situation with intense, sometimes unfair competition.
Example: "The talent show got pretty dog eat dog toward the finals."
51. dog in the manger
Meaning: Someone who stops others from enjoying something they don't even want themselves.
Example: "He won't use the extra room but won't let anyone else use it either — a real dog in the manger."
52. a dog and pony show
Meaning: A flashy presentation with a lot of style but little real substance.
Example: "The sales pitch was just a dog and pony show."
53. (as) crooked as a dog's hind leg
Meaning: Very dishonest.
Example: "Don't trust him — he's as crooked as a dog's hind leg."
Other Animals
54. like a bat out of hell
Meaning: Moving extremely fast.
Example: "He grabbed his backpack and ran like a bat out of hell to catch the bus."
55. have bats in the belfry
Meaning: To be considered eccentric or a little crazy.
Example: "Everyone thinks the inventor next door has bats in the belfry."
56. like a bear with a sore head
Meaning: Very irritable and bad-tempered.
Example: "Don't talk to him before coffee — he's like a bear with a sore head."
57. break the back of the beast
Meaning: To succeed in overcoming a major difficulty.
Example: "After weeks of work, the team finally broke the back of the beast and finished the project."
58. crocodile tears
Meaning: Fake sadness or insincere crying.
Example: "He cried crocodile tears about breaking the vase, but we knew he wasn't really sorry."
59. eat crow
Meaning: To admit you were wrong and apologize.
Example: "After his prediction failed, he had to eat crow in front of the class."
60. as the crow flies
Meaning: Measured in a straight line, not following roads or paths.
Example: "It's only two miles as the crow flies, but much farther by road."
61. like a deer/rabbit caught in the headlights
Meaning: Frozen with surprise or fear, unable to react.
Example: "When the teacher called on him, he looked like a deer caught in the headlights."

English Idioms: Animals, Birds, Fish & Insects — Part 2
More dogs, ducks, fish, flies & other creatures — an idiom module for young learners
This module continues our animal idiom collection with 66 more idioms, grouped by theme — more dog sayings, donkeys and farm birds, fish and water creatures, flies and fleas, big sharp-eyed animals, and other creature sayings. More Dog Idioms
1. every dog has its day
Meaning: Everyone gets a chance to succeed at some point in their life.
Example: "I didn't win this time, but every dog has its day."
2. give a dog a bad name
Meaning: Once someone gets a bad reputation, it's hard for them to change how others see them.
Example: "He was blamed again just because of one mistake last year — give a dog a bad name."
3. go to the dogs
Meaning: To become worse or less successful than before.
Example: "Some worried the old arcade would go to the dogs after new management took over."
4. (a) hair of the dog that bit you
Meaning: Using a small amount of what caused a problem as a remedy for it.
Example: "He joked that a little more cake might cure his stomachache — a hair of the dog that bit you."
5. help a lame dog over stile
Meaning: To help someone who is struggling or in trouble.
Example: "She always helps a lame dog over a stile, tutoring classmates who fall behind."
6. let sleeping dogs lie
Meaning: To avoid bringing up an old problem so it doesn't cause more trouble.
Example: "They've made up, so let's let sleeping dogs lie and not mention the argument again."
7. like a dog with two tails
Meaning: Extremely happy.
Example: "When she won first prize, she was like a dog with two tails."
8. love me, love my dog
Meaning: If someone truly cares about you, they accept everything and everyone connected to you too.
Example: "My best friend and I are a package deal — love me, love my dog!"
9. as quick as a dog can lick a dish
Meaning: Extremely quickly.
Example: "He finished his chores as quick as a dog can lick a dish."
10. tail wagging the dog
Meaning: A situation where something small or minor ends up controlling something much bigger and more important.
Example: "Letting the youngest sibling plan the whole vacation was a case of the tail wagging the dog."
11. old dog for a hard road
Meaning: Experience is extremely valuable for a difficult task.
Example: "They hired their most experienced teacher for the tricky class — an old dog for a hard road."
12. small dog, tall weeds
Meaning: Describes someone who doesn't have the skill or resources to handle a task.
Example: "The new intern struggled with the huge project — small dog, tall weeds."
13. (you can't) teach an old dog new tricks!
Meaning: It's hard for someone set in their ways to learn to do things differently.
Example: "Grandpa still writes checks instead of using an app — you can't teach an old dog new tricks!"
14. top dog
Meaning: The most powerful or important person in a group.
Example: "After years of hard work, she became top dog at the company."
15. why keep a dog and bark yourself?
Meaning: There's no reason to do a task yourself if someone or something else can do it for you.
Example: "He still washes dishes by hand despite owning a dishwasher — why keep a dog and bark yourself?"
16. my dogs are barking
Meaning: My feet hurt.
Example: "After the long hike, my dogs are barking!"
17. doggie bag / doggy bag
Meaning: A bag used to take home leftover restaurant food.
Example: "We couldn't finish the pizza, so we asked for a doggie bag."
Donkeys, Ducks & Farm Birds
18. donkey work
Meaning: The boring or repetitive part of a task.
Example: "I did all the donkey work while my partner presented the final report."
19. (for) donkey's years
Meaning: For a very long time.
Example: "He's lived in this neighborhood for donkey's years."
20. talk the hind leg off a donkey
Meaning: To talk a great deal, often without stopping.
Example: "It's hard to leave a conversation with her — she could talk the hind leg off a donkey!"
21. (a) dead duck
Meaning: A plan or project that has failed or been abandoned.
Example: "The fundraiser turned out to be a dead duck after low turnout."
22. (a) lame duck
Meaning: A person or organization struggling and unable to manage without help.
Example: "The small shop became a lame duck after the bigger store opened nearby."
23. (a) sitting duck
Meaning: An easy target, someone or something vulnerable to attack or trickery.
Example: "Without a helmet, cyclists are sitting ducks on busy roads."
24. get your ducks in a row
Meaning: To get everything organized and ready.
Example: "Let's get our ducks in a row before the big presentation."
25. like water off a duck's back
Meaning: Criticism that has no effect on someone at all.
Example: "The teasing was like water off a duck's back to her — she just kept smiling."
26. take to something like a duck to water
Meaning: To learn or adapt to something quickly and naturally.
Example: "She took to skateboarding like a duck to water."
27. all his geese are swans
Meaning: Someone who always exaggerates how good or important something is.
Example: "Don't believe everything he says about his trip — all his geese are swans."
28. get somebody's goat
Meaning: To annoy or irritate someone.
Example: "The constant tapping really gets my goat."
29. cook someone's goose
Meaning: To ruin someone's chances of success.
Example: "Forgetting his lines really cooked his goose in the audition."
30. have goose pimples
Meaning: To have bumpy skin from being cold or very scared.
Example: "The scary movie gave me goose pimples."
31. sauce for the goose
Meaning: What is fair or appropriate for one person should be fair for another too.
Example: "If he gets extra time, so should everyone — sauce for the goose is sauce for the gander."
32. wild goose chase
Meaning: A hopeless search for something that's very unlikely to be found.
Example: "Looking for the missing sock turned into a wild goose chase."
Fish & Water Creatures
33. (a) big fish in a small pond
Meaning: An important person within a small group, though maybe not as important elsewhere.
Example: "He's the star player at his tiny school — a big fish in a small pond."
34. a different kettle of fish
Meaning: A completely different matter from what was just discussed.
Example: "Reading about a country is one thing, but visiting it is a different kettle of fish."
35. drink like a fish
Meaning: To drink a lot of alcohol.
Example: "Uncle Joe used to drink like a fish before he decided to stop."
36. (as) dumb as a fish
Meaning: Completely silent, either unable or unwilling to speak.
Example: "He sat there dumb as a fish during the whole meeting."
37. neither fish nor fowl
Meaning: Something that's hard to classify because it doesn't clearly belong to one category or another.
Example: "The new subject is neither fish nor fowl — part art, part science."
38. other fish to fry
Meaning: To have more important things to do.
Example: "I can't help today — I've got other fish to fry."
39. other fish in the sea
Meaning: There are many other people or options just as good as the one you missed out on.
Example: "Don't worry about not making the team — there are plenty of other fish in the sea."
40. sounds/smells fishy
Meaning: Something seems suspicious or untrustworthy.
Example: "His excuse sounds fishy to me."
41. fish in troubled waters
Meaning: To try to take advantage of a difficult or chaotic situation.
Example: "Some people were fishing in troubled waters during the chaos after the storm."
42. (a) fish out of water
Meaning: To feel uncomfortable because you're in an unfamiliar situation.
Example: "As the only new kid, he felt like a fish out of water on his first day."
43. red herring
Meaning: A misleading clue or detail that distracts from the real issue.
Example: "The mystery book was full of red herrings to keep readers guessing."
Flies & Fleas
44. happy as a flea in a doghouse
Meaning: Very happy and content.
Example: "Since getting the new puppy, she's happy as a flea in a doghouse."
45. (be sent away with) a flea in your ear
Meaning: To be scolded and sent away after doing something wrong.
Example: "He was sent away with a flea in his ear after blaming his sister."
46. (a) fly in the ointment
Meaning: A small problem that spoils an otherwise good situation.
Example: "The picnic was perfect except for one fly in the ointment — no napkins!"
47. (a) fly on the wall
Meaning: To secretly watch or listen to something without being noticed.
Example: "I'd love to be a fly on the wall during their planning meeting."
48. (a) bar fly
Meaning: Someone who spends a lot of time at bars.
Example: "You'll find him at the same café every afternoon — a real bar fly for coffee."
49. (wouldn't) hurt a fly
Meaning: Describes someone who is very gentle and never causes harm.
Example: "Don't worry about the new dog — he wouldn't hurt a fly."
50. catch flies
Meaning: To have your mouth open for a long time, usually from surprise or sleepiness.
Example: "The kids stood there catching flies, amazed by the magic trick."
Big & Sharp-Eyed Creatures
51. eagle eyes
Meaning: Very good eyesight or a strong ability to notice small details.
Example: "She has eagle eyes — she spotted the typo instantly."
52. an elephant in the room
Meaning: An obvious problem that everyone notices but avoids talking about.
Example: "The failing grade was the elephant in the room during dinner."
53. memory like an elephant / an elephant never forgets
Meaning: Having an excellent memory.
Example: "Ask Grandma about that day — she has a memory like an elephant."
54. a white elephant
Meaning: Something expensive that turns out to be more trouble than it's worth.
Example: "The unused pool became a real white elephant for the family."
55. sly as a fox
Meaning: Very clever and good at tricking others.
Example: "Be careful trading cards with him — he's sly as a fox."
56. eyes like a hawk
Meaning: Having excellent eyesight and noticing every detail.
Example: "Dad has eyes like a hawk — he spots every mess before we can clean it up."
Frogs, Hares, Hogs & More
57. eat the frog / eat that frog
Meaning: To do the hardest or most unpleasant task first thing, instead of putting it off.
Example: "I hate math homework, so I always eat the frog and do it before anything else."
58. (have a) frog in one's throat
Meaning: To have difficulty speaking clearly because of a cough or sore throat.
Example: "Sorry, I have a frog in my throat this morning."
59. take/have a gander
Meaning: To take a look at something, usually something new or interesting.
Example: "Come take a gander at the new playground!"
60. knee-high to a grasshopper
Meaning: Very young and small (usually describing a child).
Example: "Grandpa says he's known me since I was knee-high to a grasshopper."
61. guinea pig
Meaning: Someone used to test a new idea, method, or product.
Example: "I volunteered to be the guinea pig for the class's new science experiment."
62. run with the hare and hunt with the hounds
Meaning: To try to stay friendly with both sides of an argument.
Example: "He can't run with the hare and hunt with the hounds forever — eventually he'll have to pick a side."
63. go the whole hog
Meaning: To do something completely and thoroughly.
Example: "For her birthday, they decided to go the whole hog with decorations, cake, and a bounce house."
64. live high off the hog
Meaning: To live a very comfortable, luxurious life.
Example: "Since he got the promotion, he's been living high off the hog."
65. hornet's nest
Meaning: A situation that causes a lot of anger or trouble when stirred up.
Example: "Bringing up the schedule change stirred up a real hornet's nest."
66. horse of a different colour
Meaning: Something completely different from what was expected or discussed before.
Example: "I thought this would be easy, but it turned out to be a horse of a different colour."
