English Idioms: Employment & Jobs
Getting hired, learning the ropes & workplace life — an idiom module for young learners
Idioms are expressions where the words together mean something different from their normal, literal meaning. In this module, you will learn 41 idioms about jobs and employment — getting and losing a job, skills and training, pay and rewards, and workplace roles and politics. Read each meaning and example carefully, then try the practice sentences at the end.
Getting & Losing a Job
1. dead end job
Meaning: A job with no future opportunities or advancement.
Example: "For now I'm stuck in a dead end job, but I'm hoping to find something better."
2. on the dole
Meaning: Receiving financial help from the government while unemployed.
Example: "Their father is on the dole, so the family is living on a tight budget."
3. get the axe
Meaning: To lose your job.
Example: "When the company restructured, several employees got the axe."
4. be sacked / get the sack
Meaning: To lose your job, usually because of something you did wrong.
Example: "He got the sack when his boss caught him lying about his hours."
5. walking papers
Meaning: Notice that you are being fired or let go from your job.
Example: "After the incident, she got her walking papers."
6. shape up or ship out
Meaning: A warning that someone must improve or they'll have to leave.
Example: "When he kept arriving late, he was told to shape up or ship out."
7. put out to pasture
Meaning: To be forced to retire or give up your responsibilities.
Example: "He felt too healthy and active to be put out to pasture just yet."
8. (a) plum job
Meaning: A desirable, well-paid position that seems relatively easy.
Example: "She landed a plum job right out of school."
9. pound the pavement
Meaning: To go from place to place searching for a job.
Example: "He's been pounding the pavement since he lost his job last month."
10. earn while you learn
Meaning: To get paid a salary while you're still in training.
Example: "The apprenticeship program lets you earn while you learn."
Skills, Training & Learning the Job
11. learn the ropes
Meaning: To learn how to do a job correctly.
Example: "It took her a week to learn the ropes at her new job."
12. show someone the ropes
Meaning: To teach someone how to do a particular job.
Example: "The manager spent the morning showing the new hire the ropes."
13. tricks of the trade
Meaning: Clever techniques or shortcuts learned through experience.
Example: "The chef taught her apprentice all the tricks of the trade."
14. another string to your bow
Meaning: An additional skill you can rely on if other plans fail.
Example: "Speaking French fluently gives her another string to her bow when job hunting."
15. square peg in a round hole
Meaning: Someone who isn't suited to the job or situation they're in.
Example: "He was a bad choice for the role — a square peg in a round hole."
16. eager beaver
Meaning: Someone who works very hard and enthusiastically, sometimes a little too much.
Example: "The new intern is a real eager beaver — first to arrive, last to leave."
17. cream of the crop
Meaning: The best people or things in a group.
Example: "The cream of the crop of this year's graduates were offered the best jobs."
18. number cruncher
Meaning: A humorous term for someone who is very good with numbers, like an accountant.
Example: "She's the office number cruncher — she handles all the budget reports."
19. worth one's salt
Meaning: Deserving respect because you do your job well.
Example: "Any inspector worth their salt would double-check the paperwork."
20. work your fingers to the bone
Meaning: To work extremely hard.
Example: "He worked his fingers to the bone to make the project a success."
21. pull your weight
Meaning: To do your fair share of the work.
Example: "It's great working with her — she always pulls her weight."
Pay, Perks & Rewards
22. golden handcuffs
Meaning: A large financial reward given to keep someone in their job.
Example: "The bonus was like golden handcuffs, keeping her at the company."
23. golden handshake
Meaning: A generous payment given to someone when they leave a job or retire.
Example: "He received a golden handshake after 30 years at the company."
24. golden parachute
Meaning: A contract clause guaranteeing large benefits if an executive's job ends.
Example: "The CEO's contract included a golden parachute in case of a takeover."
25. (be) paid peanuts
Meaning: To earn very little money.
Example: "He loves the job, but he's paid peanuts for it."
26. above and beyond the call of duty
Meaning: Doing more than what is normally expected or required.
Example: "The firefighter's actions went above and beyond the call of duty."
27. busman's holiday
Meaning: Spending your free time doing the same kind of activity as your job.
Example: "My uncle is a chef, so cooking for the family on vacation is a busman's holiday for him."
28. xerox subsidy
Meaning: A humorous term for using the office photocopier for personal use.
Example: "Printing party invitations at work is a classic xerox subsidy."
29. duty bound
Meaning: Required to do something as part of your responsibilities.
Example: "Teachers are duty bound to report a student's absence."
Workplace Roles & Politics
30. back-room boys
Meaning: People who do important work behind the scenes without public recognition.
Example: "The back-room boys don't always get credit for their hard work."
31. brain drain
Meaning: The departure of highly skilled people to other countries for better opportunities.
Example: "The country has struggled with brain drain as scientists move abroad."
32. dead wood
Meaning: People or things that are no longer useful or necessary.
Example: "The new manager wants to cut out the dead wood to reduce costs."
33. dream ticket
Meaning: Two people who would work very well together and be successful.
Example: "The two coaches teaming up would be a dream ticket for the team."
34. firing line
Meaning: A position where you're likely to be criticized because of your responsibilities.
Example: "The manager was in the firing line after the mistake was discovered."
35. glass ceiling
Meaning: An invisible barrier that keeps certain groups from reaching higher positions.
Example: "She worried she'd never break through the glass ceiling at her company."
36. play second fiddle
Meaning: To accept a lower or less important position compared to someone else.
Example: "He didn't like playing second fiddle to the new team captain."
37. separate the sheep from the goats
Meaning: To examine a group and decide who is suitable and who isn't.
Example: "The tryouts helped separate the sheep from the goats."
38. too many chiefs, not enough Indians
Meaning: A situation with too many people giving instructions and not enough people doing the work.
Example: "The project failed because there were too many chiefs and not enough Indians."
39. throw (something) over the wall
Meaning: To pass a problem to someone else without any communication or coordination.
Example: "You can't just finish your part and throw it over the wall to the next team!"
40. waiting in the wings
Meaning: Waiting for an opportunity to step in, often to replace someone.
Example: "Several talented understudies were waiting in the wings for their chance."
41. work to rule
Meaning: To do only the minimum required work as a form of protest.
Example: "In protest of the new rules, the workers decided to work to rule."
