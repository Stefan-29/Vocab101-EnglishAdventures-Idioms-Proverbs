Commonly-Used Proverbs Beginning With 'C', 'D' & 'E'
Character, effort, hope & everyday wisdom — a proverb module for young learners
Proverbs are short, well-known sayings that give advice or share a truth about life, often passed down for generations. In this module, you will learn 61 proverbs that start with the letters C, D, and E, grouped by theme — judging people, family & friendship, speech & silence, facing difficulty, diligence & success, change & human nature, and everyday life. Read each meaning and example carefully, then try the practice sentences at the end.
Judging People & Appearances
1. Clothes don't make the man.
Meaning: Appearances can be deceiving.
Example: "He wore an old jacket to the tournament, but he still played like a champion — clothes don't make the man."
2. Don't judge a book by its cover.
Meaning: Don't judge by appearances.
Example: "The plain-looking notebook turned out to have the best stories inside — don't judge a book by its cover."
3. Dogs of the same street bark alike.
Meaning: People of the same background tend to have the same behaviour.
Example: "The kids from that neighborhood all cheer for the same team the same way — dogs of the same street bark alike."
4. Every man thinks his own geese swans.
Meaning: Everyone tends to rate their own children, possessions, or achievements more highly than others do.
Example: "Every parent at the recital thought their own child sang the best — every man thinks his own geese swans."
5. Diamonds cut diamonds.
Meaning: Refers to two people equally matched in wit or cunning.
Example: "The two chess champions traded clever move after clever move — diamonds cut diamonds."
6. Children and fools tell the truth.
Meaning: Neither children nor fools know that it is sometimes better to stay quiet instead of speaking bluntly.
Example: "My little cousin announced loudly that the cake tasted burnt — children and fools tell the truth."
7. Empty vessels make the most noise.
Meaning: The least knowledgeable people are often the most talkative.
Example: "The kid who studied the least talked the loudest about the test — empty vessels make the most noise."
8. Every ass likes to hear himself bray.
Meaning: People like to listen to themselves talking.
Example: "He went on and on about his goal, even though no one was really listening — every ass likes to hear himself bray."
Family, Home & Friendship
9. Charity begins at home.
Meaning: A person's first duty is to help and care for their own family.
Example: "Before donating his allowance, he made sure his little sister had what she needed — charity begins at home."
10. Children are certain cares but uncertain comforts.
Meaning: Children are bound to cause worry but do not always bring joy in return.
Example: "Grandma sighed that raising five kids meant plenty of worry and only some quiet moments — children are certain cares but uncertain comforts."
11. A constant guest is never welcome.
Meaning: If you visit too often, your host will be less pleased to see you.
Example: "He dropped by his neighbor's house every single afternoon until they stopped answering the door — a constant guest is never welcome."
12. Distance makes the heart grow fonder.
Meaning: When you are separated from someone you love, your feelings grow stronger.
Example: "After her best friend moved away, she realized how much she missed her — distance makes the heart grow fonder."
13. An Englishman's home is his castle.
Meaning: A person's home is where they feel safest, enjoy privacy, and can do as they wish.
Example: "He loves nothing more than relaxing in his own room after a long day — an Englishman's home is his castle."
14. An empty purse frightens away friends.
Meaning: When someone's financial situation gets worse, some friends tend to disappear.
Example: "He noticed fewer invitations after spending all his savings — an empty purse frightens away friends."
Speech & Silence
15. A closed mouth catches no flies.
Meaning: If you learn when to keep silent, you will avoid trouble.
Example: "She bit her tongue instead of arguing back — a closed mouth catches no flies."
16. Don't bark if you can't bite.
Meaning: Don't complain if you can't back up your words with action.
Example: "He threatened to report the referee but never did — don't bark if you can't bite."
17. Every why has a wherefore.
Meaning: There is an explanation for everything.
Example: "She was confused about the strange rule, until the teacher explained the history behind it — every why has a wherefore."
18. Care is no cure.
Meaning: Worrying about something does not solve the problem.
Example: "He paced back and forth before the test instead of studying — care is no cure."
Facing Difficulty & Hope
19. A danger foreseen is half avoided.
Meaning: If you are prepared to face a problem, it will be easier to deal with.
Example: "They packed a spare tire before the road trip — a danger foreseen is half avoided."
20. The darkest hour is just before dawn.
Meaning: The most difficult moment often comes right before a problem is solved.
Example: "Just when the team felt hopeless, they scored the winning point — the darkest hour is just before dawn."
21. Discretion is the better part of valour.
Meaning: It is sometimes better to avoid a dangerous situation than to face it head-on.
Example: "He decided not to climb the shaky ladder after all — discretion is the better part of valour."
22. Crosses are ladders that lead to heaven.
Meaning: Suffering and misfortune often reveal the best in a person's character.
Example: "After breaking her arm, she discovered how patient and brave she could be — crosses are ladders that lead to heaven."
23. Every cloud has a silver lining.
Meaning: There is a positive or hopeful side to every situation.
Example: "The rained-out game meant they got to have a cozy movie afternoon instead — every cloud has a silver lining."
24. Every rose has its thorn.
Meaning: Every good thing has an unpleasant side.
Example: "The class trip was wonderful, but the long bus ride wasn't fun — every rose has its thorn."
25. Every path has its puddle.
Meaning: Progress is rarely without difficulty.
Example: "Learning to skateboard meant a few scraped knees along the way — every path has its puddle."
26. Death is the great leveller.
Meaning: All people are equal when they die.
Example: "The old story reminded the king that even rulers are equal to everyone else in the end — death is the great leveller."
27. Dead men tell no lies.
Meaning: A person who is gone can no longer reveal a secret.
Example: "In the old mystery book, the detective said the only witness could no longer speak — dead men tell no lies."
28. The die is cast.
Meaning: A decision has been made and it is impossible to change it.
Example: "Once she hit submit on the contest entry, there was no turning back — the die is cast."
Diligence, Effort & Success
29. Diligence is the mother of good fortune.
Meaning: Hard work brings rewards.
Example: "She practiced piano every day, and it finally paid off at the recital — diligence is the mother of good fortune."
30. Elbow grease is the best polish.
Meaning: Hard work produces the best results.
Example: "He scrubbed the old bike until it shone like new — elbow grease is the best polish."
31. The early bird catches the worm.
Meaning: Act early, or before anyone else, if you want an advantage or to be successful.
Example: "She got to the book fair right when it opened and found the best books — the early bird catches the worm."
32. Early to bed, and early to rise, makes a man healthy, wealthy and wise.
Meaning: It is much better for you to go to bed early and get up early in the morning.
Example: "He started sleeping earlier and felt so much more focused at school — early to bed, and early to rise, makes a man healthy, wealthy and wise."
33. Easier said than done.
Meaning: What is suggested sounds easy, but it is more difficult to actually do it.
Example: "Everyone said to just relax before the recital, but that was easier said than done."
34. Every man is the architect of his own fortune.
Meaning: Life is what you make it.
Example: "She built her own lemonade stand business from scratch — every man is the architect of his own fortune."
35. Constant occupation prevents temptation.
Meaning: When you are busy working, you avoid getting into trouble.
Example: "Keeping busy with the school play kept him out of trouble after class — constant occupation prevents temptation."
36. Don't bite off more than you can chew.
Meaning: Trying to do more than you can handle can lead to difficulty.
Example: "He signed up for three clubs and a sport, then felt overwhelmed — don't bite off more than you can chew."
37. Don't count your chickens before they're hatched.
Meaning: You must not be too confident that something will be successful.
Example: "She celebrated winning the spelling bee before the results were even announced — don't count your chickens before they're hatched."
38. Cross the stream where it is shallowest.
Meaning: Always do something the easiest possible way.
Example: "Instead of climbing over the fence, he found the gate — cross the stream where it is shallowest."
39. No cure, no pay.
Meaning: If the results promised are not produced, there will be no payment.
Example: "The tutor said he wouldn't charge unless her grades improved — no cure, no pay."
40. The end justifies the means.
Meaning: Sometimes unusual methods are excused if the outcome turns out well.
Example: "The coach rearranged the whole practice schedule to help the team make the finals, saying the end justifies the means."
41. Experience is the best teacher.
Meaning: We learn from our experiences.
Example: "After forgetting his lines once, he always double-checked before every play — experience is the best teacher."
42. Experience is the father of wisdom.
Meaning: Experience and knowledge result in better judgement.
Example: "Her years of babysitting taught her how to calm any crying toddler — experience is the father of wisdom."
Change, Fate & Human Nature
43. A calm sea does not make a skilled sailor.
Meaning: A person shows their competence or ability when difficulties arise.
Example: "She proved how capable she was only when the tricky final exam came around — a calm sea does not make a skilled sailor."
44. A change is as good as a rest.
Meaning: A change in routine is often as refreshing as a break or a holiday.
Example: "Switching from homework to painting felt just as relaxing as a nap — a change is as good as a rest."
45. A chain is no stronger than its weakest link.
Meaning: The strength of a group depends on each individual member.
Example: "The relay team practiced with their slowest runner the most — a chain is no stronger than its weakest link."
46. Even a worm will turn.
Meaning: Even someone who never complains will react to an intolerable situation.
Example: "He finally spoke up after his brother borrowed his things one too many times — even a worm will turn."
47. Every man for himself.
Meaning: You must think of your own interests before the interests of others.
Example: "When the last seat opened up, everyone raced for it — every man for himself."
48. Every man has his price.
Meaning: Everyone's loyalty can be tempted by the right offer.
Example: "He wouldn't trade his favorite trading card for anything — until his friend offered the rare one he'd always wanted — every man has his price."
49. Everything in the garden is rosy.
Meaning: Everything is satisfactory.
Example: "With good grades and a winning team, everything in the garden was rosy for her this year."
50. Enough is as good as a feast.
Meaning: A moderate amount is sufficient.
Example: "One slice of cake made her just as happy as a whole plate — enough is as good as a feast."
51. A cat has nine lives.
Meaning: Cats seem to survive dangerous falls and accidents again and again.
Example: "The cat tumbled off the fence and walked away without a scratch — a cat has nine lives."
52. Don't dig your grave with your own knife and fork.
Meaning: Don't cause your own downfall through your own habits, such as overeating.
Example: "Grandpa always reminds us not to overeat at every holiday dinner — don't dig your grave with your own knife and fork."
53. The devil looks after his own.
Meaning: Success or good luck sometimes comes to those who deserve it least.
Example: "The one kid who never studied somehow guessed every answer right — the devil looks after his own."
54. The devil makes work for idle hands.
Meaning: People who have no work, or are idle, often get into trouble.
Example: "Once summer break started, boredom led to a few too many pranks — the devil makes work for idle hands."
Everyday Life, Health & Weather
55. Cleanliness is next to godliness.
Meaning: A clean body and tidy habits are just as important as good character.
Example: "She always washes up before dinner, believing cleanliness is next to godliness."
56. Clear moon, frost soon.
Meaning: If the night sky is clear, frost may form soon after.
Example: "Grandpa looked at the clear moon and predicted a frosty morning — clear moon, frost soon."
57. Courtesy is contagious.
Meaning: If you are polite to other people, they will be polite to you.
Example: "She said good morning to everyone on the bus, and soon the whole bus was smiling and saying hello back — courtesy is contagious."
58. A day of sorrow is longer than a month of joy.
Meaning: Time goes by very quickly when you are happy and very slowly when you are sad.
Example: "The rainy day stuck inside felt endless compared to the fun weekend before — a day of sorrow is longer than a month of joy."
59. A drop of ink may make a million think.
Meaning: A thought expressed in writing can influence a large number of people.
Example: "Her letter to the school newspaper changed how everyone saw the recycling problem — a drop of ink may make a million think."
60. A dry March, a wet April and a cool May may fill barn and cellar and bring much hay.
Meaning: Certain weather patterns are believed to predict a good harvest.
Example: "The farmer smiled at the weather that spring, remembering the old saying about March, April, and May."
61. Diseases of the soul are more dangerous than those of the body.
Meaning: Emotional or spiritual struggles can be more harmful than physical illness.
Example: "Grandma always said feeling lonely can hurt more than a scraped knee — diseases of the soul are more dangerous than those of the body."
