English Idioms in Action!
Feelings, Emotions and Reactions — an idiom module for young learners
Idioms are expressions where the words together mean something different from their normal, literal meaning. In this module, you will learn 22 idioms that all connect to feelings, emotions and reactions — from being over the moon with happiness to feeling tongue-tied with nerves. Read each meaning and example carefully, then try the practice sentences at the end.
On Top of the World! (Happy Feelings)
1. over the moon
Meaning: To be extremely happy about something.
Example: "Mia was over the moon when she won first prize in the art competition."
2. proud as a peacock
Meaning: To feel extremely proud of something you or someone else has done.
Example: "Dad was as proud as a peacock when I scored the winning goal."
3. spring in your step
Meaning: To walk with energy and confidence because you feel happy.
Example: "Ever since she made the swim team, Aiko has had a spring in her step."
4. stars in your eyes
Meaning: To look extremely happy or excited about something.
Example: "Leo had stars in his eyes when he saw the new bike waiting for him."
Losing It! (Strong Reactions)
5. freak out
Meaning: To react with extreme excitement, fear, or surprise.
Example: "The whole class freaked out when the fire alarm suddenly rang."
6. go bananas
Meaning: To become very excited or behave in a wild way.
Example: "The crowd went bananas when the home team scored the final goal."
7. throw a wobbly
Meaning: To suddenly lose your temper, often over something small.
Example: "My little brother threw a wobbly when his tower of blocks fell down."
8. hot under the collar
Meaning: To feel annoyed, embarrassed, or angry about something.
Example: "Jake got hot under the collar when his teammate blamed him for the mistake."
Staying Calm (Keeping Control)
9. keep a level head
Meaning: To stay calm and think clearly, even in a difficult situation.
Example: "Even though the test was hard, Priya kept a level head and finished on time."
10. keep a stiff upper lip
Meaning: To stay calm and not let others see how upset you really feel.
Example: "Even though he was disappointed, Sam kept a stiff upper lip after losing the match."
11. get a grip on yourself
Meaning: To make an effort to control your feelings so you can deal with a situation.
Example: "Get a grip on yourself — we still have time to fix the poster before class."
12. pull yourself together
Meaning: To calm down and control your emotions after something upsetting.
Example: "After crying about the broken vase, Maya pulled herself together and helped clean up."
What's Going On Inside? (Showing or Hiding Feelings)
13. an open book
Meaning: To be someone whose feelings are easy to understand, because they hide nothing.
Example: "You always know how Ben feels — his face is an open book."
14. read someone like a book
Meaning: To easily understand what someone is thinking or feeling.
Example: "Grace has been my best friend for years — I can read her like a book!"
15. wear your heart on your sleeve
Meaning: To let other people easily see your feelings.
Example: "Tariq wears his heart on his sleeve — you always know when he's upset."
16. written all over your face
Meaning: To have your feelings show so clearly that everyone can see them.
Example: "Her excitement about the surprise party was written all over her face."
17. come out of your shell
Meaning: To become less shy and more confident with others.
Example: "It took a few weeks, but Noah finally came out of his shell at his new school."
Uh Oh! (Awkward Moments)
18. put your foot in your mouth
Meaning: To say something by accident that upsets or embarrasses someone.
Example: "I really put my foot in my mouth when I asked about the pet hamster — nobody had told me it had run away."
19. wish the ground would swallow you up
Meaning: To feel so embarrassed that you want to disappear.
Example: "When I tripped in front of the whole class, I wished the ground would swallow me up."
20. tongue-tied
Meaning: To have trouble speaking clearly because you feel nervous or shy.
Example: "Ali became completely tongue-tied when it was his turn to give the class presentation."
Mixed Up Feelings
21. mixed feelings
Meaning: To feel two different emotions about something at the same time.
Example: "I had mixed feelings about moving to a new school — excited but also a little sad."
22. bored to tears
Meaning: To find something so uninteresting that it feels like torture.
Example: "The students were bored to tears during the long assembly speech."
Practice Time!
Fill in each blank with the correct idiom from this module. Change the form if needed (for example, add -ed or -ing).
1. When Mei found out she won the science fair, she was ______.
2. Try to ______ before the big game — panicking won't help.
3. It's obvious how Tom feels; his happiness is ______.
4. I nearly ______ when I called my teacher “Mom” by mistake!
5. At first, Zara was very shy, but she slowly ______ at her new school.
6. Dana had ______ about switching teams — she'll miss her old friends but is excited for something new.
Answer Key (for teacher/parent)
1. over the moon
2. keep a level head
3. written all over his face
4. wished the ground would swallow me up
5. came out of her shell
6. mixed feelings
